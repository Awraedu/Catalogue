# Conditional Sentences

> **Core idea:** A **conditional sentence** describes a condition and its possible result. It usually contains an **if-clause** and a **result clause**.

## 1. Basic Structure

**If + condition, result**

* If it rains, we will stay home.
* We will stay home if it rains.

> The comma is normally used when the **if-clause comes first**.

---

## 2. Zero Conditional — General Truths

Used for **facts, rules, habits, and things that are always or generally true**.

**Structure:**

> **If + present simple, present simple**

**Examples:**

* If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.
* If students study regularly, they learn more.
* If plants do not get water, they die.

**Meaning:** The result is generally or always true.

---

## 3. First Conditional — Real Possibility

Used for a **possible future situation** and its likely result.

**Structure:**

> **If + present simple, will + base verb**

**Examples:**

* If I study hard, I will pass the exam.
* If it rains tomorrow, we will stay home.
* If she arrives early, we will start the meeting.

⚠️ Do **not** normally use *will* directly after **if**.

❌ If I will study, I will pass.
✅ If I study, I will pass.

---

## 4. Second Conditional — Unreal or Unlikely Situation

Used for **imaginary, unlikely, or hypothetical present/future situations**.

**Structure:**

> **If + past simple, would + base verb**

**Examples:**

* If I had more money, I would buy a new laptop.
* If she studied harder, she would get better results.
* If I were you, I would review my notes.

### Important: **If I were you**

For hypothetical situations, **were** is traditionally used with all subjects:

> If I were you, I would study tonight.

This expression is very common in exams and in giving advice.

---

## 5. Third Conditional — Unreal Past

Used to talk about a **past situation that did not happen** and its imaginary result.

**Structure:**

> **If + past perfect, would have + past participle**

**Examples:**

* If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.
* If she had left earlier, she would have caught the bus.
* If they had practiced, they would have won the match.

**Meaning:**

* I did not study hard.
* I did not pass.

The third conditional often expresses **regret or an imaginary past result**.

---

## 6. Quick Comparison

| Type       | If-Clause      | Result Clause   | Main Use                  |
| ---------- | -------------- | --------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Zero**   | Present simple | Present simple  | General truth             |
| **First**  | Present simple | will + verb     | Real future possibility   |
| **Second** | Past simple    | would + verb    | Unreal/unlikely situation |
| **Third**  | Past perfect   | would have + V3 | Unreal past               |

### Example with the same idea

* **Zero:** If I study, I understand the lesson.
* **First:** If I study tonight, I will pass the test.
* **Second:** If I studied more, I would get higher marks.
* **Third:** If I had studied more, I would have passed the test.

---

## 7. Other Words Used in Conditionals

Conditional sentences can also use **unless, as long as, provided that, and if**.

### Unless = if ... not

* Unless you study, you will fail.
* = If you do not study, you will fail.

### As long as = provided that

* You can use my laptop as long as you are careful.

### Provided that = only if

* You can join the trip provided that you finish your work.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Using *will* after if

❌ If she will come, I will tell her.
✅ If she comes, I will tell her.

### ❌ Confusing second and third conditionals

❌ If I had studied harder, I would pass the exam.
✅ If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.

### ❌ Forgetting the past perfect in the third conditional

❌ If I studied harder, I would have passed.
✅ If I had studied harder, I would have passed.

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

Remember the four patterns:

> **Zero:** If + present → present
> **First:** If + present → will + verb
> **Second:** If + past → would + verb
> **Third:** If + past perfect → would have + V3

**Key clue:** Ask whether the situation is **generally true, possible, imaginary, or impossible because it is already in the past.**

---

## Quick Check

1. If you heat ice, it ______.
   **Answer:** melts

2. If I study tonight, I ______ pass the exam.
   **Answer:** will

3. If I were rich, I ______ travel around the world.
   **Answer:** would

4. If she had studied, she ______ have passed.
   **Answer:** would

5. Unless you hurry, you ______ miss the bus.
   **Answer:** will

---

> **💡 Remember:**
> **Zero = fact** → *If you heat ice, it melts.*
> **First = possible** → *If I study, I will pass.*
> **Second = imaginary** → *If I studied, I would pass.*
> **Third = past regret** → *If I had studied, I would have passed.*
