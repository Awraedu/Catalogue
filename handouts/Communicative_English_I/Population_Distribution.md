# Population Distribution

> **Core idea:** **Population distribution** refers to the way people are **spread across a particular geographical area**. People are not distributed equally; some areas are highly populated while others have few inhabitants.

## 1. What Is Population Distribution?

**Population distribution** describes **where people live and how they are spread across an area**.

For example, within a country:

* Some people live in **large cities**.
* Many live in **agricultural areas**.
* Some areas have very few inhabitants.
* Some regions have populations concentrated around particular resources or services.

> **Population = how many people**
> **Population distribution = where people live**

---

## 2. Population Distribution vs. Population Density

These concepts are closely related but different.

| Concept                     | Meaning                              |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Population**              | Total number of people               |
| **Population density**      | Number of people per unit of land    |
| **Population distribution** | How people are spread across an area |

### Example

Imagine two regions:

**Region A**

* Large population concentrated in one city

**Region B**

* Similar population spread across many villages

They may have similar population sizes but **very different distributions**.

---

## 3. Patterns of Population Distribution

Population distribution can generally be described as:

### Concentrated

People are grouped in particular areas.

> Example: A large number of people living around a major city.

### Dispersed

People are spread over a wider area.

> Example: Small settlements separated by large distances.

### Uneven

Some areas have many people while other areas have very few.

> **Most populations are unevenly distributed.**

---

## 4. Factors Affecting Population Distribution

People tend to settle where living conditions and opportunities are favourable.

### Physical Factors

**Climate**

Moderate climates can be more favourable for settlement than extremely hot or cold environments.

**Water**

People need water for drinking, agriculture, industry, and other activities.

**Relief**

Flat or gently sloping land is often easier to build on and cultivate than very steep terrain.

**Soil**

Fertile soils can attract agricultural populations.

**Natural resources**

Minerals, forests, and other resources can encourage settlement and economic activity.

---

### Human Factors

**Employment**

People often move toward areas with more jobs.

**Transportation**

Roads, railways, and other transport networks can encourage settlement and economic development.

**Education and health services**

Universities, schools, hospitals, and other services can attract people.

**Urbanization**

The growth of towns and cities often causes people to move from rural areas toward urban centres.

**History and politics**

Historical settlement patterns, government policies, and political conditions can also influence where people live.

---

## 5. Population Distribution in Ethiopia

Ethiopia has **uneven population distribution**.

Population tends to be more concentrated in areas with favourable conditions for settlement, agriculture, employment, transportation, and access to services.

Some areas have lower population concentrations because of factors such as:

* Arid conditions
* Limited water
* Difficult terrain
* Remoteness
* Fewer economic opportunities

> **Exam point:** Population distribution is influenced by a combination of **physical and human factors**, not by one factor alone.

---

## 6. Why Cities Attract Population

Urban areas often have a higher concentration of people because they provide:

* Employment
* Education
* Health services
* Markets
* Transportation
* Government services
* Business opportunities

This process is closely connected with **urbanization**.

### Urbanization

**Urbanization** is the growth of urban areas and the increasing proportion of people living in towns and cities.

> **More economic opportunities → migration toward cities → greater urban population concentration**

---

## 7. Population Distribution and Migration

**Migration** is the movement of people from one place to another.

Migration can change population distribution.

### Rural → Urban

People may move to cities because of:

* Jobs
* Education
* Better services
* Business opportunities

### Rural → Rural

People may move between rural areas because of:

* Agricultural opportunities
* Land
* Water
* Environmental conditions

### Urban → Urban

People may move between cities for:

* Employment
* Education
* Business
* Family reasons

---

## 8. Push and Pull Factors

Migration is often explained using **push and pull factors**.

### Push factors

Conditions that encourage people to **leave** an area.

Examples:

* Unemployment
* Drought
* Conflict
* Lack of services
* Poverty

### Pull factors

Conditions that **attract** people to another area.

Examples:

* Employment
* Education
* Better healthcare
* Higher income
* Better infrastructure

### Easy memory

> **Push = pushes people away**
> **Pull = pulls people toward**

---

## 9. Population Distribution and Population Pyramids

A **population pyramid** is a graph that shows the **age and sex structure** of a population.

It can help us understand population characteristics and possible future changes.

The Ethiopian freshman module's Unit 5 reading specifically introduces **population pyramids** as part of its population topic.

A population pyramid can help identify:

* Proportion of young people
* Proportion of older people
* Male and female populations
* Population growth patterns

> **Population distribution** asks **where people live**, while a **population pyramid** shows important characteristics of the population, especially age and sex.

---

## 10. How to Interpret Population Information

When reading a population map, graph, or text, ask:

### **1. Where?**

Where are people concentrated?

### **2. Where are populations low?**

Which areas have fewer inhabitants?

### **3. What pattern?**

Is the population concentrated, dispersed, or uneven?

### **4. Why?**

What physical or human factors might explain the pattern?

### **5. What is changing?**

Is migration, urbanization, or economic development changing the distribution?

> **Locate → Compare → Identify the pattern → Explain the causes**

## Quick Check

1. What is population distribution?
   **Answer:** The way people are spread across a geographical area.

2. Is population distribution usually equal?
   **Answer:** No. Population is generally unevenly distributed.

3. Give three physical factors affecting population distribution.
   **Answer:** Climate, water availability, and relief.

4. Give three human factors affecting population distribution.
   **Answer:** Employment, transportation, and access to services.

5. What is urbanization?
   **Answer:** The growth of urban areas and the increasing concentration of people in towns and cities.

6. What is the difference between population density and distribution?
   **Answer:** Density measures the number of people per unit of land, while distribution describes how and where those people are spread.

7. What is a push factor?
   **Answer:** A condition that encourages people to leave a place.

8. What is a pull factor?
   **Answer:** A condition that attracts people to another place.
