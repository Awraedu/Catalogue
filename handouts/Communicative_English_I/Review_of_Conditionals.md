# Review of Conditionals

> **Core idea:** A **conditional sentence** describes a condition and its possible, imagined, or past result. It usually contains an **if-clause** and a **main clause**.

## 1. Basic Structure

A conditional has two parts:

> **If-clause + main clause**

Example:

> **If you study hard, you will pass the exam.**

* **If you study hard** → condition
* **you will pass the exam** → result

The order can change:

> **You will pass the exam if you study hard.**

### Comma rule

If the **if-clause comes first**, use a comma:

> If you study hard, you will pass.

If the **main clause comes first**, normally no comma is needed:

> You will pass if you study hard.

---

# 2. The Four Main Types

| Type       | If-clause      | Main clause     | Main meaning              |
| ---------- | -------------- | --------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Zero**   | Present Simple | Present Simple  | General truth             |
| **First**  | Present Simple | will + V1       | Real possibility          |
| **Second** | Past Simple    | would + V1      | Unlikely/unreal situation |
| **Third**  | Past Perfect   | would have + V3 | Unreal past/regret        |

> **Zero = always true**
> **First = possible**
> **Second = unlikely/imagined**
> **Third = impossible past**

---

# 3. Zero Conditional

The **Zero Conditional** describes things that are generally or always true.

### Form

> **If + Present Simple, Present Simple**

### Examples

> If you **heat** ice, it **melts**.

> If water **reaches** 100°C, it **boils**.

> If people **do not eat**, they **become** hungry.

The result is presented as a **general fact**, not a prediction about one particular future event.

### Meaning

> **Condition → general result**

### Remember

**Zero = fact**

---

# 4. First Conditional

The **First Conditional** describes a **real or likely possibility**, usually in the present or future.

### Form

> **If + Present Simple, will + V1**

### Examples

> If I **study** hard, I **will pass** the exam.

> If it **rains**, we **will stay** home.

> If you **work** hard, you **will succeed**.

The condition is possible, and the result may happen.

### Important

Do **not** normally use *will* directly after **if** for the standard first conditional.

❌ If I **will study**, I will pass.

✅ If I **study**, I will pass.

---

# 5. Other Modal Verbs in the First Conditional

The main clause does not always need **will**.

You can also use:

* can
* may
* might
* should
* ought to

### Examples

> If you study hard, you **can pass** the exam.

> If it rains, we **may stay** home.

> If you feel sick, you **should see** a doctor.

The Ethiopian FLEn 1011 material includes first-condition examples using forms such as **will, can, may, and ought to**.

---

# 6. Second Conditional

The **Second Conditional** describes an **unlikely, imaginary, or hypothetical situation** in the present or future.

### Form

> **If + Past Simple, would + V1**

### Examples

> If I **had** more money, I **would travel** around Ethiopia.

> If she **studied** harder, she **would pass** the exam.

> If I **were** you, I **would accept** the opportunity.

The condition is not presented as a likely reality.

### Meaning

> **Imagined condition → imagined result**

---

# 7. “Were” in the Second Conditional

A particularly important expression is:

> **If I were you...**

Example:

> If I **were you**, I **would study** harder.

This means:

> “If I were in your situation...”

It is commonly used when **giving advice**.

### Exam tip

For formal English, remember:

> **If I were...**

rather than:

> If I was...

---

# 8. Third Conditional

The **Third Conditional** describes an **unreal past situation**.

The condition did not happen, so the imagined result did not happen either.

### Form

> **If + Past Perfect, would have + V3**

### Examples

> If I **had studied** harder, I **would have passed** the exam.

> If she **had arrived** earlier, she **would have caught** the bus.

> If they **had left** earlier, they **would have arrived** on time.

### Meaning

> **Past condition that did not happen → imagined past result**

It can express:

* Regret
* Criticism
* Missed opportunities
* Imagined alternative past events

---

# 9. Compare the Three Main Conditional Types

### First Conditional

> If I **study**, I **will pass**.

**Meaning:** I may study, and passing is a real possibility.

### Second Conditional

> If I **studied** more, I **would pass**.

**Meaning:** Studying more is imagined or unlikely.

### Third Conditional

> If I **had studied** more, I **would have passed**.

**Meaning:** I did not study enough, and I did not pass.

### The timeline

```text
TYPE 1 → Possible future
TYPE 2 → Imagined present/future
TYPE 3 → Impossible past
```

---

# 10. Zero vs. First Conditional

These two are often confused.

### Zero

> If you **heat** ice, it **melts**.

This is a **general scientific fact**.

### First

> If you **heat** the water, it **will boil**.

This refers to a **particular possible situation**.

### Easy distinction

> **Zero = generally true**

> **First = possible in a particular situation**

---

# 11. Second vs. Third Conditional

This distinction is especially important in exams.

### Second Conditional

> If I **had** more time, I **would study** more.

The situation concerns the **present/future**.

### Third Conditional

> If I **had had** more time, I **would have studied** more.

The situation concerns the **past**.

### Memory trick

> **Second = would + V1**

> **Third = would have + V3**

---

# 12. Unless

**Unless** means approximately:

> **if...not**

### Example

> Unless you study, you will fail.

Means:

> If you **do not study**, you will fail.

Another example:

> Unless it rains, we will go outside.

Means:

> If it **does not rain**, we will go outside.

### Important

Do not normally use another negative after **unless**.

❌ Unless you **don't** study, you will fail.

✅ Unless you study, you will fail.

---

# 13. Changing the Order

The two clauses can normally change position without changing the basic meaning.

### If-clause first

> If you study hard, you will pass.

### Main clause first

> You will pass if you study hard.

The same principle applies to other conditional types.

> If I had more time, I would read more.

> I would read more if I had more time.

---

# 14. Negative Conditionals

Both clauses can be negative when appropriate.

### First Conditional

> If you **don't study**, you **won't pass**.

### Second Conditional

> If I **didn't have** work, I **wouldn't stay** home.

### Third Conditional

> If she **hadn't missed** the bus, she **wouldn't have arrived** late.

Always consider the intended meaning before making a clause negative.

---

# 15. Conditionals in Real Situations

The Ethiopian module uses everyday and academic situations to practice conditional sentences. Examples include studying, work, health, travel, and university life.

### Examples

> If students **take** good notes, they **will remember** more information.

> If I **were** more confident, I **would speak** English more often.

> If I **had reviewed** my notes, I **would have done** better on the test.

> If you **heat** water sufficiently, it **boils**.

---

# 16. How to Identify the Conditional Type

When you see an **if-clause**, check the verb forms.

### Step 1

Look at the **if-clause**.

### Step 2

Look at the **main clause**.

### Step 3

Match the forms.

| If-clause    | Main clause     | Type       |
| ------------ | --------------- | ---------- |
| Present      | Present         | **Zero**   |
| Present      | will + V1       | **First**  |
| Past         | would + V1      | **Second** |
| Past Perfect | would have + V3 | **Third**  |

---

# 17. Common Mistakes

### ❌ If I will study, I will pass.

### ✅ If I **study**, I will pass.

---

### ❌ If I would have money, I would travel.

### ✅ If I **had** money, I would travel.

---

### ❌ If I had studied, I would pass.

### ✅ If I had studied, I **would have passed**.

---

### ❌ If she had went earlier, she would have arrived.

### ✅ If she had **gone** earlier, she would have arrived.

---

### ❌ Unless you don't work hard, you will fail.

### ✅ Unless you work hard, you will fail.

---

# 18. Conditional Forms at a Glance

> **ZERO**

`If + present → present`

> If you heat ice, it melts.

---

> **FIRST**

`If + present → will + V1`

> If I study, I will pass.

---

> **SECOND**

`If + past → would + V1`

> If I studied, I would pass.

---

> **THIRD**

`If + had + V3 → would have + V3`

> If I had studied, I would have passed.

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

The Ethiopian FLEn 1011 module specifically asks students to review conditionals by focusing on **form, use, and meaning**, and to produce examples for Types I, II, and III.

### Memorize this table

| Type       | Condition       | Result            | Meaning            |
| ---------- | --------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ |
| **Zero**   | `if + V1`       | `V1`              | General truth      |
| **First**  | `if + V1`       | `will + V1`       | Real possibility   |
| **Second** | `if + V2`       | `would + V1`      | Unlikely/imaginary |
| **Third**  | `if + had + V3` | `would have + V3` | Unreal past        |

### Fast exam trick

> **Present → Present = ZERO**

> **Present → Will = FIRST**

> **Past → Would = SECOND**

> **Had + V3 → Would have + V3 = THIRD**

## Quick Check

### 1. Choose the correct answer:

If you heat ice, it ______.

A. will melt
B. melted
C. melts
D. would melt

**Answer: C — melts**

---

### 2. Choose the correct answer:

If I study hard, I ______ the exam.

A. pass
B. would pass
C. will pass
D. would have passed

**Answer: C — will pass**

---

### 3. Choose the correct answer:

If I had more money, I ______ a new laptop.

A. will buy
B. would buy
C. bought
D. would have bought

**Answer: B — would buy**

---

### 4. Choose the correct answer:

If she had studied harder, she ______ the exam.

A. will pass
B. would pass
C. would have passed
D. passes

**Answer: C — would have passed**

---

### 5. Identify the type:

> If water reaches 100°C, it boils.

**Answer:** **Zero Conditional**

---

### 6. Identify the type:

> If I meet him, I will tell him.

**Answer:** **First Conditional**

---

### 7. Identify the type:

> If I were you, I would apologize.

**Answer:** **Second Conditional**

---

### 8. Identify the type:

> If they had left earlier, they would have caught the bus.

**Answer:** **Third Conditional**

---

### 9. Rewrite using **unless**:

> If you do not study, you will fail.

**Answer:**

> **Unless you study, you will fail.**

---

**Answer:**

> **If I had known, I would have helped you.**
