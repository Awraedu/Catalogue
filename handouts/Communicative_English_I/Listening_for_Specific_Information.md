# Listening for Specific Information

> **Core idea:** **Listening for specific information** means listening carefully to find **particular facts or details** such as names, numbers, dates, places, reasons, times, or key words.

## 1. What Is Specific Information?

Specific information is a **particular detail** rather than the general meaning of a whole conversation or lecture.

You may need to listen for:

* **Names** → Who?
* **Places** → Where?
* **Dates and times** → When?
* **Numbers** → How many? How much?
* **Reasons** → Why?
* **Actions** → What happened?
* **Descriptions** → What is it like?
* **Examples** → For example, what?

### Example

> “The meeting will be held on **Monday at 10:30 a.m. in Room 204**.”

Specific information includes:

* **Day:** Monday
* **Time:** 10:30 a.m.
* **Place:** Room 204

---

## 2. Listening for Keywords

You do not need to understand **every word** to find specific information.

Focus on **keywords** connected to the question.

### Example

Question:

> **Where will the students meet?**

Listen for words related to a **place**:

> “The students will meet at the **main library**.”

**Answer:** The main library.

---

## 3. Predict Before Listening

Before listening, read the questions and predict what type of information you need.

| Question     | Listen for   |
| ------------ | ------------ |
| Who...?      | Person/name  |
| Where...?    | Place        |
| When...?     | Date/time    |
| How many...? | Number       |
| Why...?      | Reason       |
| What...?     | Action/fact  |
| How much...? | Amount/price |

> **Prediction helps your brain know what to listen for.**

---

## 4. Listen for Signal Words

Speakers often use words or phrases that introduce important information.

### Examples

* **The important point is...**
* **For example...**
* **The answer is...**
* **It happened in...**
* **The reason is...**
* **Remember that...**
* **The first...**
* **Finally...**
* **In particular...**

These expressions can signal that useful information is coming.

---

## 5. Listen for Numbers and Names

Numbers and names are common targets in listening activities.

Pay special attention to:

* Dates
* Telephone numbers
* Prices
* Percentages
* Measurements
* Ages
* Names of people
* Names of places

### Example

> “The population increased from **2.5 million to 3.2 million**.”

Important details:

* Original population → **2.5 million**
* New population → **3.2 million**

---

## 6. Watch for Corrections

Speakers sometimes give information and then **correct it**.

> “The meeting is on Tuesday—sorry, I mean **Wednesday**.”

The correct answer is **Wednesday**.

Listen carefully for expressions such as:

* **Sorry, I mean...**
* **Actually...**
* **No, that's not correct...**
* **Let me correct that...**
* **I mean...**

---

## 7. Take Short Notes

Do not try to write every word.

Write only the information you need.

### Example

Instead of:

> “The lecturer said that students should submit their assignments before Friday afternoon.”

Write:

> **Assignment → Friday afternoon**

Useful symbols:

* **→** leads to / means
* **+** and
* **−** decrease
* **↑** increase
* **?** question
* **★** important

---

## 8. A Simple Listening Strategy

### **Before listening**

* Read the questions.
* Underline keywords.
* Predict the type of answer.

### **While listening**

* Focus on the required information.
* Listen for keywords and signal words.
* Write short notes.
* Do not panic if you miss one word.

### **After listening**

* Check your answers.
* Review numbers, names, and dates.
* Make sure your answer matches the question.

> **Read → Predict → Listen → Note → Check**

---

## 9. Common Problems

### Trying to understand every word

This can make you miss the important information.

**Better:** Focus on the information required by the question.

### Losing focus after missing one answer

Do not stop listening.

**Better:** Move on and continue listening.

### Confusing similar information

Speakers may mention several numbers, names, or places.

**Better:** Connect each detail to its context.

### Writing too much

Long notes can make you miss what comes next.

**Better:** Use short words and symbols.

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

In a listening test, **specific information questions usually have clear answers**.

Look for:

> **Who + What + Where + When + Why + How much/how many**

### Example

Audio:

> “The university opened its new laboratory in **2024**. The laboratory is located near the **Science Faculty** and can accommodate **150 students**.”

Possible questions:

* When did it open? → **2024**
* Where is it located? → **Near the Science Faculty**
* How many students can it accommodate? → **150**

## Quick Check

1. What is specific information?
   **Answer:** A particular fact or detail from what you hear.

2. What should you do before listening?
   **Answer:** Read the questions, identify keywords, and predict the information needed.

3. What should you do if you miss one answer?
   **Answer:** Continue listening instead of losing focus.

4. What type of information does **“When?”** usually require?
   **Answer:** A date or time.

5. Why should you take short notes?
   **Answer:** To record important details without missing the next information.

---

> **💡 Remember:**
> **Don't listen to everything equally. Listen for what the question is asking for.**
