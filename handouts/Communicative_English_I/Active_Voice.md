# Active Voice

> **Core idea:** In the active voice, the **subject performs the action**.

---

## 1. What Is Active Voice?

A sentence is in the **active voice** when the person or thing doing the action comes before the verb.

### Basic structure

```text
Subject + Verb + Object
```

### Example

> **The student** completed **the assignment**.

* **Subject:** The student
* **Verb:** completed
* **Object:** the assignment

The student **does** the action.

---

## 2. Active vs. Passive Voice

Compare these sentences:

| Active Voice                        | Passive Voice                              |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| The teacher explained the lesson.   | The lesson was explained by the teacher.   |
| Students completed the assignment.  | The assignment was completed by students.  |
| The researcher conducted the study. | The study was conducted by the researcher. |
| The lecturer asked a question.      | A question was asked by the lecturer.      |

The meaning is similar, but the **focus is different**.

### Active

> **The researcher** conducted the study.

Focus → **the researcher**

### Passive

> **The study** was conducted by the researcher.

Focus → **the study**

> **Tip:** Active voice usually makes sentences shorter, clearer, and more direct.

---

# 3. How to Identify Active Voice

Ask:

> **"Who or what performs the action?"**

If the subject performs the action, the sentence is active.

### Example

> **Abebe wrote the report.**

Who performed the action?

→ **Abebe**

Therefore, the sentence is **active**.

### Another example

> **The computer processes the information.**

Who performs the action?

→ **The computer**

Therefore, it is active voice.

---

# 4. Active Voice in Different Tenses

Active voice can be used in different English tenses.

| Tense                  | Example                                 |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Present Simple**     | Students read books.                    |
| **Past Simple**        | Students read the book yesterday.       |
| **Future Simple**      | Students will read the book tomorrow.   |
| **Present Continuous** | Students are reading the book.          |
| **Present Perfect**    | Students have completed the assignment. |

The important point is that **the subject performs the action**.

---

# 5. Changing Passive to Active

To change a passive sentence into active voice:

### Step 1

Find the person or thing that performed the action.

### Step 2

Put it at the beginning.

### Step 3

Use the appropriate verb tense.

### Step 4

Put the receiver of the action after the verb.

### Example

**Passive:**

> The assignment was completed by the students.

**Active:**

> The students completed the assignment.

### Another example

**Passive:**

> The experiment was conducted by the researchers.

**Active:**

> The researchers conducted the experiment.

---

# 6. Why Use Active Voice?

Active voice is useful because it often makes writing:

* **Clearer**
* **Shorter**
* **More direct**
* **Easier to understand**
* **More natural**

### Compare

**Passive:**

> The decision was made by the committee.

**Active:**

> The committee made the decision.

The active sentence is more direct.

---

# 7. Active Voice in Academic Writing

Active voice is common in academic writing when the writer wants to make responsibility clear.

### Example

> **The researchers analyzed the data.**

This clearly tells us who performed the action.

Another example:

> **The study examines the relationship between language and culture.**

Active voice can make academic writing easier to follow.

However, **passive voice is not wrong**. Academic writers use both voices depending on what they want to emphasize.

---

# 8. Common Mistake

Do not confuse **active voice** with simply having a verb.

Look at:

> The book was written by the student.

The book does not perform the action.

The **student** wrote the book.

Therefore, the sentence is **passive**, not active.

Active version:

> **The student wrote the book.**

# ⭐ Exam Focus

### Active voice

> **Subject → performs the action**

**Example:**

> The lecturer explained the lesson.

### Passive voice

> **Subject → receives the action**

**Example:**

> The lesson was explained by the lecturer.

### Quick identification rule

Ask:

> **"Who is doing the action?"**

If the answer is the **subject**, the sentence is active.
