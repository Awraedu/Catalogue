# Speaking About Wildlife

> **Core idea:** Speaking about wildlife means describing wild animals and their habitats, explaining their importance, discussing threats and conservation, and expressing opinions clearly and respectfully.

## 1. What Is Wildlife?

**Wildlife** refers to animals, plants, and other living organisms that live in natural environments rather than being domesticated.

When discussing wildlife, you can talk about:

* Wild animals
* Habitats
* Biodiversity
* Conservation
* Endangered species
* Human–wildlife interaction
* Wildlife tourism
* Environmental protection

### Example

> **Ethiopia has diverse wildlife because its ecosystems range from highlands and forests to grasslands and lowland areas.**

---

# 2. Wild Animals in Ethiopia

Ethiopia is home to many species of wildlife.

Examples include:

* Ethiopian wolf
* Gelada
* Walia ibex
* African elephant
* Lion
* Leopard
* Giraffe
* Hippopotamus
* Baboon
* Crocodile

Some species are particularly important because they are **endemic**, meaning they are naturally found in a particular geographical area.

### Speaking example

> **The Ethiopian wolf is an important species because it is endemic to Ethiopia and is closely associated with the country's highland ecosystems.**

---

# 3. Why Is Wildlife Important?

Wildlife is important for several reasons.

### Ecological importance

Animals contribute to healthy ecosystems.

> Predators help regulate populations, while many animals contribute to seed dispersal and other ecological processes.

### Economic importance

Wildlife can support:

* Tourism
* Employment
* Local businesses
* National income

### Educational and scientific importance

Wildlife provides opportunities for:

* Research
* Environmental education
* Scientific study

### Cultural importance

Wild animals can have cultural and symbolic importance for communities.

---

# 4. Wildlife and Tourism

**Wildlife tourism** involves traveling to observe wild animals and natural environments.

Examples include:

* Wildlife viewing
* Safari tourism
* Visiting national parks
* Birdwatching
* Nature photography

### Speaking example

> **Wildlife can benefit Ethiopia's tourism industry by attracting visitors who want to see its unique animals and natural landscapes.**

The FLEn 1011 wildlife activities specifically ask students to discuss **how Africa can benefit from its wildlife**.

---

# 5. Human–Wildlife Interaction

**Human–wildlife interaction** refers to ways in which people and wild animals come into contact and affect one another.

Some interactions are positive:

> Conservation and tourism can benefit both people and wildlife.

Others can be negative:

> Wild animals may damage crops or threaten livestock.

### Example

> **When human settlements expand into wildlife habitats, people and animals may come into greater contact.**

---

# 6. Human–Wildlife Conflict

**Human–wildlife conflict** occurs when interactions between people and wildlife result in negative consequences.

Examples include:

* Crop destruction
* Livestock predation
* Property damage
* Competition for water
* Injury to people
* Killing of wildlife in retaliation

### Speaking example

> **Human–wildlife conflict can occur when agricultural land expands into wildlife habitats. Animals may enter farms and damage crops, creating economic losses for farmers.**

---

# 7. Causes of Human–Wildlife Conflict

Common causes include:

* Habitat loss
* Deforestation
* Agricultural expansion
* Urbanization
* Competition for water
* Expansion of settlements
* Climate and environmental changes

### Useful expression

> **One major cause of human–wildlife conflict is...**

### Example

> **One major cause is habitat loss, which forces animals to move closer to human settlements in search of food and water.**

---

# 8. Threats to Wildlife

Wildlife can be threatened by:

* Habitat destruction
* Illegal hunting
* Illegal wildlife trade
* Pollution
* Climate change
* Deforestation
* Loss of food and water
* Human–wildlife conflict

### Example

> **Habitat destruction is a serious threat because animals may lose the food, shelter, and breeding areas they need to survive.**

---

# 9. Endangered Animals

An **endangered species** is a species facing a high risk of extinction.

Useful expressions:

> **The species is endangered.**

> **Its population is declining.**

> **The species is threatened by habitat loss.**

> **Conservation efforts are needed to protect it.**

### Example

> **Endangered species need effective protection because the loss of one species can affect the wider ecosystem.**

---

# 10. Protecting Wildlife

Wildlife conservation means protecting animals, plants, habitats, and ecosystems.

Possible measures include:

* Protecting natural habitats
* Establishing protected areas
* Preventing illegal hunting
* Restoring damaged habitats
* Creating wildlife corridors
* Supporting local communities
* Educating the public
* Enforcing environmental laws
* Promoting responsible tourism

### Speaking example

> **Wildlife can be protected by conserving habitats, preventing illegal hunting, educating communities, and involving local people in conservation programs.**

---

# 11. The Role of Local Communities

Conservation is more effective when local communities are involved.

Communities can:

* Protect nearby wildlife
* Report illegal activities
* Participate in conservation programs
* Benefit from responsible tourism
* Help reduce human–wildlife conflict

### Example

> **Local communities should be involved in conservation because they live close to many wildlife habitats and are directly affected by wildlife-related issues.**

---

# 12. Wildlife and the Economy

Wildlife can contribute to economic development through:

> **Wildlife → Tourism → Jobs → Local income → Economic benefits**

### Example

> **Wildlife tourism can create employment for guides, drivers, hotel workers, park staff, and local businesses.**

However, wildlife should be managed responsibly so that economic benefits do not destroy the natural resources that attract visitors.

---

# 13. Expressing Your Opinion

Wildlife topics often require you to give your opinion.

Useful expressions:

> **In my opinion,...**

> **I believe that...**

> **I think that...**

> **From my point of view,...**

> **Personally, I think...**

### Example

> **In my opinion, protecting wildlife is important because healthy ecosystems support both nature and human communities.**

---

# 14. Giving Reasons

Support your opinion with a reason.

### Weak

> Wildlife should be protected.

### Better

> Wildlife should be protected **because it is an important part of healthy ecosystems.**

Useful connectors:

* because
* since
* therefore
* as a result
* for this reason
* so

---

# 15. Giving Examples

Useful expressions:

> **For example,...**

> **For instance,...**

> **One example is...**

> **Such as...**

### Example

> **Wildlife can benefit local communities. For example, tourism can create jobs for people living near national parks.**

---

# 16. Agreeing and Disagreeing

### Agreeing

> **I agree.**

> **I completely agree.**

> **That's a good point.**

> **I think so too.**

### Disagreeing politely

> **I see your point, but...**

> **I'm not sure I agree.**

> **I understand what you mean, but...**

> **I have a different view.**

### Example

> **A:** Wildlife tourism is always beneficial.

> **B:** I see your point, but tourism needs to be managed responsibly to avoid disturbing wildlife.

---

# 17. Comparing Ideas

You may be asked to compare conservation and human needs.

Useful expressions:

> **On the one hand,...**

> **On the other hand,...**

> **However,...**

> **In contrast,...**

> **Similarly,...**

### Example

> **On the one hand, communities need land for agriculture. On the other hand, wildlife needs natural habitat to survive. Therefore, effective land-use planning is important.**

---

# 18. Talking About Solutions

When discussing a wildlife problem, use:

> **Problem → Cause → Solution → Result**

### Example

**Problem:**

> Crop damage caused by wild animals.

**Cause:**

> Loss of wildlife habitat.

**Solution:**

> Protect habitats and create appropriate barriers or wildlife corridors.

**Result:**

> Reduced conflict between farmers and wildlife.

This is an excellent structure for longer speaking answers.

> **What threatens wildlife?**

> **How can wildlife be protected?**

> **What causes human–wildlife conflict?**

# 20. Answering a Wildlife Speaking Question

### Question:

> **How can Africa benefit from its wildlife?**

### Strong answer:

> **Africa can benefit from its wildlife through tourism, employment, education, research, and conservation. For example, national parks can attract tourists who spend money on accommodation, transportation, guides, and other services. Therefore, responsible wildlife tourism can contribute to local and national economic development while encouraging conservation.**

---

# 21. A Strong Speaking Structure

Use:

> **I → R → E → S**

### **I — Idea**

State your main point.

### **R — Reason**

Explain why.

### **E — Example**

Give a specific example.

### **S — Significance**

Explain why the point matters.

### Example

> **Idea:** Wildlife should be protected.
>
> **Reason:** It is important for healthy ecosystems.
>
> **Example:** Predators help maintain ecological balance.
>
> **Significance:** Protecting wildlife therefore helps maintain biodiversity and ecosystem health.

# 24. Common Speaking Mistakes

### ❌ “Wildlife are important for tourism.”

### ✅ “Wildlife **is** important for tourism.”

When **wildlife** means animals collectively, it is generally treated as an uncountable singular noun.

---

### ❌ “Animals needs protection.”

### ✅ “Animals **need** protection.”

---

### ❌ “We should to protect wildlife.”

### ✅ “We should **protect** wildlife.”

After **should**, use the base verb.

---

### ❌ “Wild animals are useful because they make tourism.”

### ✅

> **Wild animals can support tourism by attracting visitors to natural areas.**

---

### ❌ “All wild animals are dangerous.”

### ✅

> **Some wild animals can be dangerous, particularly when people and animals come into close contact.**

Avoid unnecessary generalizations.

## Quick Check

### 1. What is wildlife?

**Answer:** Wild animals, plants, and other organisms living in natural environments.

### 2. What is a habitat?

**Answer:** The natural home or environment where an organism lives.

### 3. Give two benefits of wildlife.

**Answer:** Wildlife can support tourism and employment, contribute to ecosystems, and provide educational and scientific value.

### 4. What is human–wildlife conflict?

**Answer:** A negative interaction between people and wildlife that causes harm or loss to people, animals, or both.

### 5. Give two causes of human–wildlife conflict.

**Answer:** Habitat loss and agricultural or settlement expansion.

### 6. How can wildlife be protected?

**Answer:** By protecting habitats, preventing illegal hunting, restoring ecosystems, educating communities, and supporting conservation programs.

### 7. Complete:

> Wildlife tourism can create ______ for local communities.

**Answer:** **employment/jobs**

### 8. Give a polite way to disagree.

**Answer:** “I understand your point, but I see it differently.”

### 9. Complete the speaking structure:

> Idea → Reason → ______ → Significance

**Answer:** **Example**

### 10. Answer briefly:

> **How can Africa benefit from wildlife?**

**Possible answer:**

> Africa can benefit from wildlife through tourism, employment, education, research, and conservation.
