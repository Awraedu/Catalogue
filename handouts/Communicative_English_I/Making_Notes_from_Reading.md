# Making Notes from Reading

> **Core idea:** **Making notes from reading** means identifying the most important information in a text and recording it in a **short, organized form** for later study, review, or summary writing.

## 1. Why Make Notes?

Good notes help you:

* Remember important information
* Understand difficult ideas
* Review lessons quickly
* Prepare for examinations
* Organize information
* Write summaries and assignments
* Separate **main ideas** from unnecessary details

The Ethiopian freshman English course includes note-making as an important **study skill for independent learning**.

---

## 2. What Should You Note?

Do **not** copy the entire text.

Focus on:

* **Main ideas**
* Important supporting points
* Key terms and definitions
* Important facts
* Causes and effects
* Examples
* Dates and numbers
* Comparisons
* Conclusions

### Example

**Original:**

> Population density refers to the number of people living in a particular unit of land area. It is usually expressed as people per square kilometre.

**Notes:**

> **Population density** → number of people / unit of land
> **Unit:** people/km²

The notes contain the important information without copying the whole paragraph.

---

## 3. Main Ideas vs. Details

The most important skill is knowing what information deserves to be recorded.

### Main idea

The **central point** of a paragraph or section.

### Supporting detail

Information that **explains, proves, or gives an example** of the main idea.

### Example

> **Main idea:** Regular exercise improves health.
> **Details:** It strengthens the heart, improves fitness, and can reduce the risk of some diseases.

A good note might be:

> **Exercise → improves health**
> → strengthens heart
> → improves fitness
> → reduces disease risk

---

## 4. Use Your Own Words

Avoid copying complete sentences from the source.

### Original

> “Deforestation is the removal of forests on a large scale, often caused by agriculture, logging, and settlement.”

### Poor notes

> “Deforestation is the removal of forests on a large scale, often caused by agriculture, logging, and settlement.”

### Better notes

> **Deforestation** → large-scale removal of forests
> **Causes:** agriculture, logging, settlement

> **Exam tip:** Notes should normally be **shorter than the original text** and written mainly in your own words.

---

## 5. Use Keywords and Phrases

Notes do not always need complete sentences.

### Instead of:

> “Population growth can increase pressure on natural resources because more people require food, water, and energy.”

Write:

> **Population growth → greater pressure on resources**
> → food
> → water
> → energy

This makes your notes **faster to write and easier to review**.

---

## 6. Use Abbreviations and Symbols

Short forms save time and space.

| Symbol | Meaning                |
| ------ | ---------------------- |
| →      | leads to / causes      |
| ↑      | increase               |
| ↓      | decrease               |
| =      | is / means             |
| ≠      | different from         |
| +      | and / positive         |
| −      | negative / decrease    |
| e.g.   | for example            |
| vs.    | versus / compared with |
| %      | percentage             |

### Example

> **Urbanization ↑ → population density ↑**

## 7. Organize Your Notes

Good notes should have a clear structure.

A useful format is:

```text
MAIN TOPIC
│
├── Main idea 1
│   ├── detail
│   └── example
│
├── Main idea 2
│   ├── detail
│   └── cause/effect
│
└── Main idea 3
    └── conclusion
```

You can also use:

* Headings
* Subheadings
* Bullet points
* Numbered lists
* Tables
* Arrows
* Indentation

---

## 8. A Simple Note-Making Process

### **1. Preview**

Read the **title, headings, and introduction**.

### **2. Read**

Read the text carefully.

### **3. Identify**

Find the **main ideas and important details**.

### **4. Shorten**

Remove unnecessary words and examples that are not important.

### **5. Rewrite**

Put the information into **your own words**.

### **6. Organize**

Arrange the notes logically.

### **7. Review**

Read your notes again and check whether they contain the essential information.

> **Preview → Read → Identify → Shorten → Rewrite → Organize → Review**

---

## 9. Notes for Summary Writing

Notes are especially useful when writing a **summary**.

The process is:

> **Read → Identify main points → Make notes → Connect ideas → Write summary**

The notes should contain the **main points**, not every sentence from the original text.

### Example

**Text:**
A paragraph explains that urbanization is increasing because people move from rural areas to cities in search of employment, education, and better services.

**Notes:**

> **Urbanization ↑**
> → rural → urban migration
> → jobs
> → education
> → better services

These notes can later be turned into complete sentences for a summary.

---

## 10. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Copying everything

You end up with another version of the textbook.

### ❌ Writing too little

Important ideas may be missing.

### ❌ Using complete sentences everywhere

This makes note-taking slow.

### ❌ Mixing unrelated ideas

Your notes become difficult to review.

### ❌ Ignoring the source structure

Follow the main headings and logical order of the text.

### ❌ Forgetting your own words

Copied sentences can make understanding and remembering harder.

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

When asked to **make notes from a reading passage**, remember:

### **MAIN IDEA + KEY DETAILS**

Look for:

* **Who?**
* **What?**
* **Where?**
* **When?**
* **Why?**
* **How?**
* Causes
* Effects
* Examples
* Important facts

### Best note-making rule

> **Shorter + organized + accurate + your own words**

## Quick Check

1. What is the main purpose of making notes from reading?
   **Answer:** To record important information in a short, organized form for study and review.

2. Should you copy every sentence from the text?
   **Answer:** No. Record the main ideas and important details.

3. Why should you use your own words?
   **Answer:** It helps you understand and remember the information.

4. What is the difference between a main idea and a supporting detail?
   **Answer:** The main idea is the central point; supporting details explain or support it.

5. Give two symbols that can be used in note-making.
   **Answer:** For example, **→** and **↑**.

---

> **💡 Remember:**
> **Good notes are not a shorter copy of the textbook. They are a clear map of its most important ideas.**
