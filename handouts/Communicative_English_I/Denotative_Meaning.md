# Denotative Meaning

> **Core idea:** **Denotative meaning** is the **literal, direct, and dictionary meaning** of a word, without the feelings or associations that the word may suggest.

## 1. What Is Denotative Meaning?

The denotative meaning of a word is its basic meaning—the meaning you would normally find in a **dictionary**.

### Example

The word **snake** denotes:

> A long, legless reptile.

That is its **denotative meaning**.

However, the word may also suggest ideas such as **danger, fear, or dishonesty**. Those are **connotative meanings**.

---

## 2. Denotation vs. Connotation

| Denotative Meaning    | Connotative Meaning           |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Literal meaning       | Associated meaning            |
| Dictionary definition | Feelings or ideas             |
| Usually objective     | May be subjective             |
| More stable           | Can vary by culture or person |

### Example: **Home**

* **Denotation:** A place where a person lives.
* **Connotation:** Comfort, safety, family, and belonging.

### Example: **Childish**

* **Denotation:** Relating to or resembling a child.
* **Connotation:** Immature or silly.

---

## 3. Why Denotative Meaning Is Important

Understanding denotation helps you:

* understand the **exact meaning** of words
* avoid misunderstanding
* use vocabulary accurately
* interpret academic texts
* distinguish literal meaning from implied meaning
* answer vocabulary questions correctly

In academic writing, denotative meaning is especially important because writers generally need **clear and precise language**.

---

## 4. Examples

| Word         | Denotative Meaning               |
| ------------ | -------------------------------- |
| **Rose**     | A type of flowering plant        |
| **Home**     | A place where someone lives      |
| **Snake**    | A legless reptile                |
| **Cheap**    | Low in price                     |
| **Youthful** | Young or characteristic of youth |
| **Father**   | A male parent                    |
| **School**   | An institution for education     |

The dictionary meaning stays relatively stable even though the **connotations** may differ.

---

## 5. How to Identify Denotative Meaning

When asked for the denotative meaning of a word:

1. Ignore emotional associations.
2. Think about the **literal meaning**.
3. Imagine the word's **dictionary definition**.
4. Choose the meaning that is most direct and objective.

### Example

> "The athlete was **determined** to win."

The denotative meaning of **determined** is:

> Having made a firm decision or showing strong resolve.

The positive feeling associated with the word is its **connotation**, not its denotation.

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

Remember:

> **Denotation = direct, literal, dictionary meaning.**
> **Connotation = additional feeling, association, or idea.**

### Easy Memory Trick

**D = Dictionary = Denotation**

If the question asks for the **denotative meaning**, look for the **literal definition**, not the emotion or association.

   **Answer:** A place where someone lives.

4. What is the difference between denotation and connotation?
