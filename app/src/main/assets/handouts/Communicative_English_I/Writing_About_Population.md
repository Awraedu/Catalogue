# Writing About Population

> **Core idea:** Writing about population means describing the **number, distribution, density, growth, and characteristics of people in a particular area**, then explaining important patterns, causes, and effects.

## 1. What Is Population?

**Population** means the total number of people living in a particular place at a particular time.

A population can refer to:

* A country
* A region
* A city
* A continent
* The whole world

### Example

> **Ethiopia has a large and diverse population distributed across different regions and environments.**

When writing about population, it is important to explain **what place and what period** you are discussing.

---

# 2. What Can We Write About?

Population writing can focus on several related ideas:

* Population size
* Population growth
* Population distribution
* Population density
* Birth rate
* Death rate
* Migration
* Age structure
* Population pyramids
* Urbanization
* Population changes

### Simple pattern

> **What? → Where? → How much? → Why? → What are the effects?**

---

# 3. Population Size

**Population size** refers to the total number of people living in a particular area.

### Example

> **The population of a city can increase as a result of natural population growth and migration.**

When comparing population sizes, use appropriate comparison language.

### Useful expressions

* has a large population
* has a small population
* the population increased
* the population decreased
* the population remained stable
* a rapidly growing population
* a declining population

---

# 4. Population Growth

**Population growth** is an increase in the number of people living in an area over time.

Population growth is influenced mainly by:

* Births
* Deaths
* Immigration
* Emigration

### Basic relationship

> **Population change = Births − Deaths + Immigration − Emigration**

### Example

> **A population may grow rapidly when the number of births and immigrants is greater than the number of deaths and emigrants.**

---

# 5. Population Distribution

**Population distribution** refers to **where people live and how they are spread across an area**.

Population is rarely distributed equally.

Some areas may be:

* Densely populated
* Moderately populated
* Sparsely populated

### Example

> **People tend to concentrate in areas where water, fertile land, employment, transportation, and other services are available.**

---

# 6. Population Density

**Population density** describes the number of people living in a given area of land.

### Formula

> **Population density = Total population ÷ Land area**

It is commonly expressed as:

> **people per square kilometre (people/km²)**

### Example

If an area has:

* Population = 100,000
* Area = 500 km²

Then:

> **Density = 100,000 ÷ 500 = 200 people/km²**

The Ethiopian FLEn 1011 module specifically introduces population density as a geographical concept and defines it in relation to population and area.

---

# 7. Population Density vs Population Distribution

These two terms are related but different.

| Term                        | Meaning                               |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Population size**         | How many people live in an area       |
| **Population distribution** | Where people are located              |
| **Population density**      | How many people live per unit of area |

### Remember

> **Distribution = Where?**

> **Density = How many per area?**

---

# 8. Population Pyramid

A **population pyramid** is a graph that shows the **age and sex structure of a population**.

It usually shows:

* Males on one side
* Females on the other
* Younger ages at the bottom
* Older ages at the top

Population pyramids can help us understand whether a population is:

* Young
* Stable
* Aging

The Ethiopian FLEn 1011 curriculum specifically includes **Population Pyramid** as the reading topic in Unit 5.

# 10. Describing Trends

Use adverbs to show the speed or degree of change.

### Slow change

> **The population increased gradually.**

### Fast change

> **The population increased rapidly.**

### Large change

> **The population rose significantly.**

### Small change

> **The population increased slightly.**

### No major change

> **The population remained relatively stable.**

---

# 11. Causes of Population Change

Population changes for several reasons.

### Natural increase

When births exceed deaths, the population increases naturally.

### Migration

People may move because of:

* Employment
* Education
* Conflict
* Environmental conditions
* Better services
* Family reasons

### Economic development

Economic opportunities can attract people to cities and other areas.

### Environmental factors

Water availability, climate, fertile soil, and natural resources can influence where people live.

---

# 12. Effects of Population Growth

Population growth can create both **opportunities and challenges**.

### Possible benefits

* Larger workforce
* Larger markets
* More human resources
* Increased economic activity

### Possible challenges

* Pressure on housing
* Greater demand for food and water
* Pressure on schools and hospitals
* Unemployment
* Traffic congestion
* Environmental degradation
* Increased demand for land

> **Population growth itself is not automatically good or bad; its effects depend on resources, planning, and development.**

---

# 13. Population and Urbanization

**Urbanization** is the growth of the proportion of people living in towns and cities.

People may move to urban areas because of:

* Employment opportunities
* Education
* Healthcare
* Better infrastructure
* Business opportunities

### Example

> **Rapid urbanization can increase demand for housing, transportation, water, and other public services.**

# 15. Use Data Carefully

When writing about population statistics, distinguish between:

### Fact

> **The population increased from 5 million to 6 million.**

### Interpretation

> **This increase may create greater demand for public services.**

### Prediction

> **The population may continue to increase in the future.**

Do not present predictions or interpretations as established facts.

---

# 16. Writing About a Population Graph

When describing a graph or table, follow this order:

### 1. Introduce the data

> **The graph shows population changes between 2000 and 2025.**

### 2. Give the main trend

> **Overall, the population increased during the period.**

### 3. Give important details

> **The most rapid growth occurred after 2010.**

### 4. Make comparisons

> **The population in Region A was higher than that of Region B.**

### 5. Conclude

> **Overall, the data indicate a steady increase in population.**

# 17. Model Paragraph

### Population Growth

> **Population growth is an important demographic issue because it affects the economic, social, and environmental conditions of a country.** A population can increase when births and immigration exceed deaths and emigration. Rapid population growth may increase the demand for food, water, housing, education, healthcare, and employment. However, a growing population can also provide a larger workforce and a larger market for goods and services. **Therefore, effective planning and sustainable use of resources are important for managing the opportunities and challenges associated with population growth.**

---

# 18. Model Paragraph: Population Distribution

> **Population distribution is not uniform because people prefer environments that provide suitable living and economic conditions.** Areas with reliable water supplies, fertile land, employment opportunities, transportation, schools, and healthcare often attract more people. In contrast, extremely dry, cold, mountainous, or otherwise difficult environments may have fewer inhabitants. As a result, some regions are densely populated while others are sparsely populated. **Understanding population distribution helps governments plan infrastructure, public services, and resource management.**

---

# 19. A Strong Paragraph Formula

For an exam, remember:

> **T — D — E — E — C**

### **T — Topic**

Introduce the population issue.

### **D — Definition**

Explain the key term.

### **E — Explanation**

Explain the causes, pattern, or process.

### **E — Example**

Give evidence or an example.

### **C — Conclusion**

State the main implication.

### Example

> **Population density is an important geographical concept.**
> ↓
> **It refers to the number of people living per unit of land area.**
> ↓
> **Density varies because people are not distributed equally.**
> ↓
> **For example, urban areas often have higher densities than remote rural areas.**
> ↓
> **Therefore, population density is useful for understanding how people are distributed across space.**

# 20. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Confusing population size and population density

> “The country has a high population density because it has many people.”

Having many people does not automatically mean high density.

**Density also depends on land area.**

---

### ❌ Listing data without interpretation

> 2000: 5 million
> 2010: 6 million
> 2020: 8 million

### ✅

> **The population increased from 5 million in 2000 to 8 million in 2020, with the most noticeable growth occurring after 2010.**

---

### ❌ Using incorrect trend verbs

> “The population decreased up.”

### ✅

> **The population increased.**

or

> **The population rose.**

---

### ❌ Making unsupported conclusions

> “The population will definitely double.”

### ✅

> **The population may continue to increase if current demographic trends continue.**

---

### ❌ Repeating the same word

> The population increased. The population increased rapidly. The population increased again.

### ✅

> The population **rose**, **grew**, and **increased significantly**.
