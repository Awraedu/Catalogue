# Passive Voice

> **Core idea:** The **passive voice** focuses on the **receiver of an action** rather than the person or thing that performs it.

## 1. Active vs. Passive Voice

### Active Voice

The subject **does** the action.

> **The researcher collected the data.**

* Subject → the researcher
* Verb → collected
* Object → the data

### Passive Voice

The subject **receives** the action.

> **The data was collected by the researcher.**

* Subject → the data
* Passive verb → was collected
* Doer → the researcher

### Basic pattern

> **Active:** Subject + Verb + Object
> **Passive:** Object + form of **be** + Past Participle (+ by + doer)

The Ethiopian freshman materials describe the passive as being formed with a form of **be + past participle**.

---

## 2. Why Use the Passive Voice?

We use the passive when:

### 1. The receiver is more important

> **The examination results were published yesterday.**

The results are more important than the person who published them.

### 2. The doer is unknown

> **My phone was stolen.**

We do not know who stole it.

### 3. The doer is obvious or unimportant

> **Students are expected to follow the rules.**

There is no need to mention who expects them.

### 4. We want an objective or formal style

Passive voice is common in **academic, scientific, and formal writing**.

> **The experiment was conducted in the laboratory.**

---

## 3. How to Change Active into Passive

Follow these steps:

### Step 1: Find the object

> The teacher **explained the lesson**.

Object → **the lesson**

### Step 2: Move the object to the subject position

> **The lesson** ...

### Step 3: Choose the correct form of **be**

The original tense determines the form of **be**.

### Step 4: Change the main verb to its **past participle (V3)**

> explain → **explained**

### Step 5: Add **by + doer** when necessary

> **The lesson was explained by the teacher.**

### Formula

> **Object + be + V3 (+ by + subject)**

---

## 4. Passive Voice in Different Tenses

| Tense                  | Active                      | Passive                                  |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Present Simple**     | She writes the report.      | The report **is written** by her.        |
| **Present Continuous** | She is writing the report.  | The report **is being written** by her.  |
| **Present Perfect**    | She has written the report. | The report **has been written** by her.  |
| **Past Simple**        | She wrote the report.       | The report **was written** by her.       |
| **Past Continuous**    | She was writing the report. | The report **was being written** by her. |
| **Past Perfect**       | She had written the report. | The report **had been written** by her.  |
| **Future Simple**      | She will write the report.  | The report **will be written** by her.   |
| **Modal**              | She must write the report.  | The report **must be written** by her.   |

> **Exam shortcut:** Keep the **same tense** when changing from active to passive.

---

## 5. The Most Important Passive Patterns

### Present Simple

> **am / is / are + V3**

**Active:** They produce coffee.
**Passive:** Coffee **is produced** by them.

### Past Simple

> **was / were + V3**

**Active:** They built the bridge.
**Passive:** The bridge **was built** by them.

### Present Continuous

> **am / is / are + being + V3**

**Active:** They are repairing the road.
**Passive:** The road **is being repaired**.

### Present Perfect

> **has / have + been + V3**

**Active:** They have completed the project.
**Passive:** The project **has been completed**.

### Future Simple

> **will + be + V3**

**Active:** They will announce the results.
**Passive:** The results **will be announced**.

### Modal Verbs

> **modal + be + V3**

**Active:** Students must submit the assignment.
**Passive:** The assignment **must be submitted**.

---

## 6. When Do We Use “By”?

**By + agent** tells us who performed the action.

> The novel was written **by the author**.

But **by** is often unnecessary.

> The window was broken.

We don't need to say:

> The window was broken **by someone**.

### Usually omit the agent when:

* We don't know who did it.
* The person is obvious.
* The person is unimportant.
* The focus is on the action/result.

---

## 7. Verbs Must Be Transitive

Normally, only verbs that take an **object** can be changed into the passive.

### Transitive

> They **built the house**.

Object → **the house**

Passive:

> The house **was built**.

### Intransitive

> He **arrived**.

There is no object, so a normal passive form is not possible.

---

## 8. Passive with Two Objects

Some verbs can take two objects, such as:

* give
* send
* show
* tell
* offer
* teach

### Example

**Active:**

> The teacher gave **the student** **a book**.

Two passive forms are possible:

> **The student was given a book.**

or

> **A book was given to the student.**

---

## 9. Passive Voice in Academic Writing

Passive voice is especially useful when the **process or result** is more important than the researcher.

### Example

**Active:**

> We collected the data from 500 students.

**Passive:**

> The data was collected from 500 students.

The passive version places attention on **the data**, not **we**.

However, passive voice should not be used in every sentence. Academic writing can appropriately use **both active and passive voice** depending on what the writer wants to emphasize.

---

## 10. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Forgetting “be”

> The report written yesterday.

### ✅ Correct

> The report **was written** yesterday.

---

### ❌ Using the wrong verb form

> The report was **write** yesterday.

### ✅ Correct

> The report was **written** yesterday.

---

### ❌ Changing the tense

**Active:** They **completed** the work.

❌ The work **is completed** by them.

✅ The work **was completed** by them.

---

### ❌ Adding “by” unnecessarily

> The thief was arrested **by the police**.

This is grammatically correct, but if the police are not important, simply:

> The thief was arrested.

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

When changing **active → passive**, remember:

> **OBJECT → SUBJECT**
> **VERB → BE + V3**
> **SUBJECT → BY + AGENT**

### Example

**Active:**

> The university **published** the results.

**Passive:**

> The results **were published** by the university.

### Quick method

**1. Find the object**
**2. Move it to the front**
**3. Keep the tense**
**4. Add BE**
**5. Use V3**
**6. Add BY + doer if needed**

## Quick Check

### 1. Change to passive:

> They built a new laboratory.

**Answer:** A new laboratory **was built** by them.

### 2. Change to passive:

> Students are completing the assignment.

**Answer:** The assignment **is being completed** by the students.

### 3. Change to passive:

> The university will announce the results.

**Answer:** The results **will be announced** by the university.

### 4. Which is passive?

A. The researcher collected the data.
B. The data was collected by the researcher.

**Answer:** **B**

### 5. Why might the agent be omitted?

**Answer:** Because the doer is unknown, obvious, unimportant, or less important than the action/receiver.
