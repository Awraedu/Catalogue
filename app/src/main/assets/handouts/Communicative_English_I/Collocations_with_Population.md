# Collocations with Population

> **Core idea:** A collocation is a combination of words that commonly occur together. Learning population collocations helps you speak and write about people, communities, and demographic change more naturally.

---

## 1. What Is a Collocation?

A **collocation** is a group of words that are commonly used together.

For example:

> **population growth** ✅

We normally say **population growth**, not *population increase* in every situation, although *population increase* is also possible.

Learning common combinations makes your English more natural and easier to understand.

---

# 2. Common Population Collocations

### Population + noun

| Collocation                 | Meaning                                           |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **population growth**       | Increase in the number of people                  |
| **population decline**      | Decrease in the number of people                  |
| **population increase**     | Growth in population size                         |
| **population density**      | Number of people living in a particular area      |
| **population distribution** | How people are spread across an area              |
| **population size**         | Total number of people                            |
| **population structure**    | Composition of a population                       |
| **population change**       | Change in the size or composition of a population |

### Examples

> Ethiopia has experienced significant **population growth**.

> Urban areas often have high **population density**.

> Migration can affect **population distribution**.

# 3. Verbs Used with Population

Certain verbs commonly appear with **population**.

| Verb + population | Example                                     |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **increase**      | The population increased rapidly.           |
| **decrease**      | The population decreased slightly.          |
| **grow**          | The population continues to grow.           |
| **decline**       | The population declined over several years. |
| **rise**          | The urban population rose significantly.    |
| **fall**          | The population fell after the disaster.     |
| **reach**         | The population reached five million.        |
| **exceed**        | The population exceeded the estimate.       |

### Useful patterns

> **The population increased by 10%.**

> **The population increased to 10 million.**

These two structures have different meanings:

**increased by** → tells you the **amount or percentage of change**

**increased to** → tells you the **new total**

# 5. Population Density

**Population density** describes how many people live in a particular area.

A simple formula is:

$$
\text{Population Density} =
\frac{\text{Population}}{\text{Land Area}}
$$

### Example

If 100,000 people live in an area of 500 km²:

$$
\frac{100,000}{500} = 200
$$

The population density is:

**200 people per km²**

> **High population density** → many people in a small area.

> **Low population density** → fewer people in a large area.

---

# 6. Population Growth

**Population growth** refers to an increase in the number of people in a population.

Population growth can be affected by:

* Birth rates
* Death rates
* Immigration
* Emigration

### Example

> High birth rates can contribute to rapid **population growth**.

### Related collocations

* **rapid population growth**
* **slow population growth**
* **population growth rate**
* **population growth trends**

---

# 7. Population Distribution

**Population distribution** refers to how people are spread across a geographical area.

Population is usually not distributed equally.

Some areas may have:

> **high population concentration**

while others may have:

> **low population concentration**

Factors affecting population distribution include:

* Climate
* Water availability
* Employment
* Transportation
* Natural resources
* Fertile land
* Urban development

---

# 8. Population and Migration

Migration can change the size and distribution of populations.

Useful collocations include:

* **population migration**
* **rural-to-urban migration**
* **population movement**
* **migration patterns**
* **migrant population**
* **population displacement**

### Example

> Rural-to-urban migration can increase the **urban population**.

---

# 9. Useful Academic Expressions

These expressions are common in academic writing and reports.

> **The population has increased significantly.**

> **The population is expected to grow.**

> **There has been a rapid increase in population.**

> **The population is concentrated in urban areas.**

> **The population is unevenly distributed.**

> **The region has a high population density.**

> **Population growth has slowed in recent years.**
