# Academic Vocabulary and Study Language

> **Core idea:** Academic vocabulary is the language used in university study, lectures, textbooks, assignments, and examinations.

---

## 1. What Is Academic Vocabulary?

**Academic vocabulary** consists of words commonly used in education and formal study.

You will encounter these words in:

* Lectures
* Textbooks
* Assignments
* Examinations
* Academic discussions
* Study materials

For example:

> **Identify** the main causes of climate change.

Here, *identify* is an academic instruction word. It tells you **what you must do**.

---

# 2. Common Academic Instruction Words

Understanding instruction words is especially important in examinations.

| Word          | What it means                         | Example                      |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Define**    | Give the exact meaning                | Define communication.        |
| **Describe**  | Give details about something          | Describe the process.        |
| **Explain**   | Make an idea clear                    | Explain why it is important. |
| **Identify**  | Name or point out                     | Identify two causes.         |
| **List**      | Give items one after another          | List three advantages.       |
| **Compare**   | Show similarities                     | Compare the two methods.     |
| **Contrast**  | Show differences                      | Contrast the two theories.   |
| **Discuss**   | Explain and consider important points | Discuss the effects.         |
| **Analyze**   | Examine parts and relationships       | Analyze the argument.        |
| **Evaluate**  | Judge using evidence or reasons       | Evaluate the method.         |
| **Summarize** | Give only the main points             | Summarize the passage.       |

> **Exam tip:** Before answering a question, identify the **instruction word**. It tells you what kind of answer is expected.

# 3. Words for Understanding Information

These words often appear in lectures and textbooks.

### Important verbs

* **Understand** — know the meaning of something.
* **Interpret** — explain the meaning of information.
* **Recognize** — identify something you already know.
* **Demonstrate** — show that something is true or understood.
* **Illustrate** — explain something using an example or visual.
* **Apply** — use knowledge in a particular situation.

### Example

> **Apply** the principles of communication to the situation below.

This does **not** mean simply defining communication. You must **use what you know** in the given situation.

### Cause

* Due to
* Since
* As a result of
* Caused by

### Effect

* Therefore
* Thus
* Consequently
* As a result
* Leads to
* Results in

### Example

```text
Poor communication
       ↓
Misunderstanding
       ↓
```

# 7. Formal vs. Informal Language

University study usually requires more **formal language** than everyday conversation.

| Informal   | More academic            |
| ---------- | ------------------------ |
| A lot of   | Many / Numerous          |
| Get        | Obtain / Receive         |
| Show       | Demonstrate              |
| Find out   | Determine                |
| Talk about | Discuss                  |
| Look at    | Examine                  |
| Help       | Assist                   |
| Start      | Begin                    |
| End        | Conclude                 |
| Thing      | Factor / Issue / Element |

### Example

**Informal:**

> The study looked at how students learn.

**Academic:**

> The study **examined** how students learn.

You do not need complicated words to sound academic. **Clear and precise language is more important than difficult vocabulary.**

# 9. Understanding Exam Questions

Academic vocabulary is especially important in examinations.

Consider this question:

> **Compare** verbal and non-verbal communication.

The word **compare** tells you to discuss their **similarities**.

Now consider:

> **Contrast** verbal and non-verbal communication.

The focus is their **differences**.

And:

> **Explain** the importance of feedback in communication.

You should give a **clear explanation**, not simply define feedback.

### Quick rule

```text
DEFINE     → What does it mean?
DESCRIBE   → What is it like?
EXPLAIN    → Why / How?
COMPARE    → How are they similar?
CONTRAST   → How are they different?
ANALYZE    → How do the parts relate?
EVALUATE   → How good / useful / important is it?
SUMMARIZE  → What are the main points?
```

# 10. Building Academic Vocabulary

Do not try to memorize hundreds of words at once.

Use a simple strategy:

### 1. Notice

Pay attention to unfamiliar words in lectures and reading.

### 2. Record

Write the word and its meaning.

### 3. Use

Create your own sentence with the word.

### 4. Review

Return to the word later.

### Example

```text
Word: Analyze

Meaning: Examine something carefully.

Example:
Students analyze the causes of the problem.
```

> **The best way to learn vocabulary is to use it, not simply memorize it.**

# 11. Word Families

Many academic words belong to **word families**.

Learning related forms can increase your vocabulary quickly.

| Verb        | Noun          | Adjective     |
| ----------- | ------------- | ------------- |
| Analyze     | Analysis      | Analytical    |
| Develop     | Development   | Developed     |
| Communicate | Communication | Communicative |
| Educate     | Education     | Educational   |
| Differ      | Difference    | Different     |
| Explain     | Explanation   | Explanatory   |

### Example

> Scientists **analyze** the data.

> The **analysis** shows an important pattern.

Recognizing word families can help you understand unfamiliar words when reading.

# ⭐ Exam Focus

Remember these high-value instruction words:

**Define** → give the meaning.

**Describe** → give details.

**Explain** → make clear how or why.

**Identify** → name or point out.

**Compare** → show similarities.

**Contrast** → show differences.

**Analyze** → examine carefully.

**Evaluate** → make a judgment.

**Summarize** → give the main points.

> **Always identify the command word before answering an examination question.**
