# Vocabulary in Context

> **Core idea:** You can often understand an unfamiliar word by studying the **words, sentences, and ideas around it**. This is called using **vocabulary in context**.

## 1. What Is Vocabulary in Context?

**Vocabulary in context** means understanding the meaning of an unfamiliar word from the information surrounding it.

### Example

> The road was **slippery** because it had been raining all night. Several cars moved slowly to avoid accidents.

Even if you do not know *slippery*, the context suggests:

> **slippery = difficult to walk or drive on safely because the surface is smooth or wet**

You use the surrounding information as a clue.

---

# 2. Why Is Context Important?

You will often meet unfamiliar words when reading academic texts.

Instead of stopping at every unknown word:

> **Read → Look for clues → Guess → Check**

Using context helps you:

* Read more quickly
* Understand unfamiliar vocabulary
* Continue reading without interruption
* Develop independent vocabulary-learning skills
* Understand words according to their actual use

Ethiopian FLEn 1011 materials specifically present context clues as a strategy for dealing with unfamiliar vocabulary and emphasize using **prior knowledge and contextual clues** to make reasonable guesses.

---

# 3. Context Clues

A **context clue** is information surrounding an unfamiliar word that helps you work out its meaning.

Important types include:

1. **Background knowledge**
2. **Definition**
3. **Example**
4. **Comparison**
5. **Contrast**
6. **Punctuation**
7. **Restatement**

These clue types are also identified in British Council teaching material developed in Ethiopia.

---

# 4. Background Knowledge

Sometimes your existing knowledge helps you understand an unfamiliar word.

### Example

> I could not sleep because my neighbor's dog was **yapping** all night.

You know that dogs can make loud sounds that disturb people.

Therefore:

> **yapping → making repeated barking sounds**

### Strategy

Ask:

> **What do I already know about this situation?**

Your knowledge of the world can provide an important clue.

---

# 5. Definition Clues

Sometimes the writer directly explains the unfamiliar word.

### Example

> **Stamina** is the ability to continue physical activity for a long time.

Therefore:

> **stamina = ability to continue an activity for a long time**

### Common signals

Look for:

* **means**
* **refers to**
* **is**
* **is defined as**
* **in other words**
* **that is**

### Example

> A **sedentary** lifestyle, **meaning a lifestyle with little physical activity**, may affect health.

> **sedentary = involving little physical activity**

---

# 6. Example Clues

Examples can reveal the meaning of an unfamiliar word.

### Example

> Many **aerobic activities**, such as **running, swimming, and cycling**, improve cardiovascular fitness.

The examples help you understand that aerobic activities are:

> **types of physical exercise**

### Common signals

> **for example**

> **for instance**

> **such as**

> **including**

### Exam tip

When you see examples, ask:

> **What do all these examples have in common?**

---

# 7. Comparison Clues

A writer may compare an unfamiliar word with something familiar.

### Example

> A **sprint** is short and fast, whereas a marathon requires **endurance** over a much longer distance.

The comparison suggests:

> **endurance = ability to continue for a long time**

### Common signals

* similarly
* likewise
* compared with
* as...as
* whereas

---

# 8. Contrast Clues

Sometimes an opposite or different idea helps you understand a word.

### Example

> Unlike his **active** brother, Dawit is **sedentary** and spends most of his free time sitting.

The contrast helps us infer:

> **sedentary = not physically active**

### Common signals

* **unlike**
* **but**
* **however**
* **in contrast**
* **whereas**
* **while**

---

# 9. Punctuation Clues

Punctuation can introduce explanations or definitions.

Look for:

* Commas
* Dashes
* Parentheses
* Colons

### Example

> The athlete experienced **fatigue — extreme physical tiredness —** after the race.

Therefore:

> **fatigue = extreme physical tiredness**

### Another example

> **Hydration (providing the body with enough water)** is important during exercise.

> **hydration = providing enough water to the body**

---

# 10. Restatement Clues

Sometimes the writer says the same idea in different words.

### Example

> The area was **densely populated**. In other words, many people lived there.

The restatement gives the meaning:

> **densely populated = containing many people**

### Common signals

> **in other words**

> **that is**

> **to put it another way**

> **meaning**

---

# 11. Cause-and-Effect Clues

The cause or result of an event can help reveal an unfamiliar word.

### Example

> The athlete became **dehydrated** after exercising for several hours without drinking water. He was extremely thirsty and needed fluids.

From the context:

> **dehydrated = lacking enough water**

### Think:

> **What caused it?**

> **What happened because of it?**

---

# 12. Use Several Clues Together

The best guesses often come from more than one clue.

### Example

> After running for two hours under the hot sun without drinking water, the athlete became **dehydrated**. He was extremely thirsty and needed to drink several bottles of water.

Clues:

* Hot weather
* Long exercise
* No water
* Extreme thirst
* Needed fluids

Therefore:

> **dehydrated = lacking sufficient water**

---

# 13. Guess the Part of Speech

Grammar can help you determine the meaning.

### Example

> Regular exercise is **beneficial** to health.

The word describes exercise, so it is an **adjective**.

From the context:

> **beneficial = helpful or useful**

### Common word endings

| Ending    | Often indicates |
| --------- | --------------- |
| **-tion** | noun            |
| **-ment** | noun            |
| **-ness** | noun            |
| **-ful**  | adjective       |
| **-less** | adjective       |
| **-ly**   | adverb          |
| **-ize**  | verb            |

These are useful clues, but they are not absolute rules.

---

# 14. Don't Guess Too Quickly

Do not use only the sentence containing the unknown word.

### Weak strategy

> Unknown word → immediate guess

### Better strategy

> **Read sentence → Read surrounding sentences → Find clues → Guess**

### Example

> The patient's condition was **persistent**. The symptoms continued for several weeks despite treatment.

The second sentence gives the clue:

> **persistent = continuing for a long time**

---

# 15. Guess the Meaning Needed for the Passage

You do not always need the exact dictionary definition.

### Example

> The researcher obtained **substantial** evidence to support the argument.

Even if you do not know the exact definition, you can infer:

> **substantial = considerable / large / significant**

That may be enough to understand the passage.

> **The goal is understanding, not memorizing dictionary definitions.**

---

# 16. A Step-by-Step Method

Use **C-L-U-E**:

### **C — Context**

Read the whole sentence and surrounding sentences.

### **L — Look**

Look for definitions, examples, contrasts, punctuation, and other signals.

### **U — Use**

Use your background knowledge and grammar clues.

### **E — Estimate**

Make the most reasonable meaning and check whether it fits.

### Memory line

> **CONTEXT → CLUE → GUESS → CHECK**

---

# 17. Context Clue Signals

| Signal                | Likely clue               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **means / refers to** | Definition                |
| **for example**       | Example                   |
| **such as**           | Example                   |
| **in other words**    | Restatement               |
| **unlike**            | Contrast                  |
| **however**           | Contrast                  |
| **similarly**         | Comparison                |
| **whereas**           | Comparison/contrast       |
| **because**           | Cause                     |
| **therefore**         | Effect/result             |
| **— / ( )**           | Explanation or definition |

# 18. Example: Health and Fitness

> Regular exercise can **enhance** physical fitness. It can make the body stronger and improve endurance.

What does **enhance** mean?

The context gives:

> make stronger + improve endurance

Therefore:

> **enhance = improve**

---

# 19. Example: Population

> Urban areas are often **densely populated**. In other words, many people live within a relatively small area.

What does **densely populated** mean?

> **A place where many people live in a relatively small area.**

Clue type:

> **Restatement**

---

# 20. Example: Wildlife

> Some wild animals are **endangered**, meaning that their populations have become so small that they are at risk of extinction.

What does **endangered** mean?

> **At risk of extinction**

Clue type:

> **Definition**

---

# 21. Context vs Dictionary

A dictionary is useful, but it should not always be your first step.

### Try context first when:

* The word is not essential.
* The surrounding text gives clear clues.
* You only need the general meaning.

### Use a dictionary when:

* The word is central to the text.
* Your guess is uncertain.
* You need the exact meaning.
* You want pronunciation, spelling, or usage information.

### Best strategy

> **Guess first → verify when necessary.**

---

# 22. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Looking up every unfamiliar word

This slows down reading.

### ✅ Try context first.

---

### ❌ Looking at only one sentence

### ✅ Read the surrounding sentences.

---

### ❌ Ignoring signal words

### ✅ Pay attention to words such as **however, such as, means, unlike, and in other words**.

---

### ❌ Making a random guess

### ✅ Base your guess on evidence from the context.

---

### ❌ Searching for the exact dictionary definition

### ✅ First identify the meaning needed to understand the passage.

# Quick Check

### 1. What is vocabulary in context?

**Answer:** Understanding an unfamiliar word by using information surrounding it.

### 2. What does “such as” usually introduce?

**Answer:** Examples.

### 3. What does “unlike” usually signal?

**Answer:** Contrast.

### 4. Identify the clue type:

> Hydration, **the process of providing the body with enough water**, is important during exercise.

**Answer:** **Definition**

### 5. Guess the meaning:

> The athlete was exhausted after the marathon and could barely walk.

**Answer:** **Very tired**

### 6. Guess the meaning:

> Unlike active students, sedentary students spend most of their time sitting.

**Answer:** **Not physically active**

### 7. Identify the clue type:

> Many aerobic activities, such as swimming and cycling, improve fitness.

**Answer:** **Example**

### 8. What should you do if your first guess does not fit the sentence?

**Answer:** Re-read the surrounding context, reconsider the clues, and make a new guess or check a dictionary.

### 9. Do you always need the exact dictionary definition?

**Answer:** No. A reasonable meaning that helps you understand the passage is often enough.

### 10. Complete:

> **Context → ______ → Guess → Check**

**Answer:** **Clue**
