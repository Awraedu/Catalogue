# Types of Conditionals

> **Core idea:** Conditional sentences describe a **condition** and its possible or imagined **result**. They usually contain an **if-clause** and a **main clause**.

## 1. What Is a Conditional?

A conditional sentence shows that one situation depends on another.

### Basic structure

> **If + condition, result.**

### Example

> **If I study hard, I will pass the exam.**

* **If I study hard** = condition
* **I will pass the exam** = result

The order can be changed:

> **I will pass the exam if I study hard.**

When the **if-clause comes first**, use a comma.

When the **main clause comes first**, normally no comma is needed.

---

# 2. The Three Main Types

For Ethiopian FLEn 1011, the key conditional types are:

| Type         | Main use        | If-clause      | Main clause     |
| ------------ | --------------- | -------------- | --------------- |
| **Type I**   | Real/possible   | Present simple | will + V1       |
| **Type II**  | Unreal/unlikely | Past simple    | would + V1      |
| **Type III** | Unreal past     | Past perfect   | would have + V3 |

> **Memory trick:**
> **I = Possible**
> **II = Imaginary**
> **III = Past/Impossible**

The FLEn 1011 module specifically asks students to focus on the **form, use, and meaning** of each conditional type.

---

# 3. Type I — Real or Possible Condition

### Use

Type I describes a condition that is **possible or likely** in the present or future.

### Form

> **If + present simple, will + base verb**

### Examples

> If I **study**, I **will pass**.

> If it **rains**, we **will stay** home.

> If you **work** hard, you **will succeed**.

> If tourists **visit** the park, they **will see** wild animals.

The FLEn 1011 module uses Type I in contexts such as meeting a sportsperson and predicting what will happen if tourists visit national parks.

### Important

Do **not** normally use **will** directly after *if* for the ordinary Type I conditional.

❌ If I **will study**, I will pass.

✅ If I **study**, I will pass.

---

# 4. Type II — Unreal or Unlikely Condition

### Use

Type II describes a situation that is:

* Unreal
* Imaginary
* Unlikely
* Hypothetical

It is often about the present or future.

### Form

> **If + past simple, would + base verb**

### Examples

> If I **had** more money, I **would travel**.

> If I **were** you, I **would study** harder.

> If she **worked** harder, she **would succeed**.

> If I **met** Zinedine Zidane, I **would ask** him about his career.

The Ethiopian FLEn 1011 materials describe Type II as expressing a **possible but unlikely** condition.

### Important: “were”

In formal hypothetical English, **were** is commonly used with all subjects:

> If I **were** you, I would accept the opportunity.

> If he **were** here, he would help us.

This is especially common in the expression:

> **If I were you,...**

---

# 5. Type III — Unreal Past Condition

### Use

Type III describes an **imaginary past situation**—something that did not happen and therefore had a different result.

It is often used for:

* Regret
* Criticism
* Imagining a different past
* Explaining an alternative past result

### Form

> **If + past perfect, would have + past participle**

### Examples

> If I **had studied**, I **would have passed** the exam.

> If she **had arrived** earlier, she **would have caught** the bus.

> If they **had worked** harder, they **would have succeeded**.

### Meaning

> I didn't study → I didn't pass.

The Type III conditional imagines what **could have happened differently in the past**. The FLEn 1011 materials describe it as an impossible past condition.

---

# 6. Compare the Three Types

Consider the same idea in three forms:

### Type I

> **If I study, I will pass.**

Meaning:

> Studying is possible, and passing is a possible future result.

### Type II

> **If I studied, I would pass.**

Meaning:

> Studying is hypothetical or unlikely.

### Type III

> **If I had studied, I would have passed.**

Meaning:

> I didn't study, so I didn't pass.

### The key difference

> **Type I → Possible**

> **Type II → Imaginary/Unlikely**

> **Type III → Impossible past**

---

# 7. A Simple Timeline

### Type I

**Now → Future**

> If I study → I will pass.

### Type II

**Present/Future imagination**

> If I studied → I would pass.

### Type III

**Past → Alternative past**

> If I had studied → I would have passed.

---

# 8. Negative Conditionals

Conditionals can also be negative.

### Type I

> If you **don't study**, you **won't pass**.

### Type II

> If I **didn't have** work, I **would travel**.

### Type III

> If she **hadn't missed** the bus, she **wouldn't have been** late.

---

# 9. “Unless”

**Unless** means approximately:

> **if ... not**

### Example

> Unless you study, you will fail.

=

> If you **don't** study, you will fail.

### Another example

> Unless it rains, we will play football.

=

> If it **doesn't** rain, we will play football.

### Important

Don't use an unnecessary negative after **unless**.

❌ Unless you **don't** study, you will fail.

✅ Unless you study, you will fail.

---

# 10. Modal Verbs in Conditionals

The main clause does not always need **will** or **would**.

Other modal verbs can be used when appropriate.

### Type I

> If you study hard, you **can** pass.

> If it rains, we **may** stay home.

> If you are sick, you **should** see a doctor.

### Type II

> If I had more time, I **could** help you.

> If she studied harder, she **might** pass.

### Type III

> If he had left earlier, he **could have caught** the bus.

---

# 11. Conditional Questions

Conditionals are useful for asking hypothetical questions.

### Type I

> **What will you do if you pass the exam?**

> If I pass, I will celebrate with my friends.

### Type II

> **What would you do if you had a million birr?**

> If I had a million birr, I would start a business.

### Type III

> **What would you have done if you had missed the exam?**

> If I had missed the exam, I would have contacted my instructor.

The FLEn 1011 module uses questions involving meeting famous Ethiopian athletes and asking what students **will do, would do, or would have done** in different situations.

# 15. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Type I

> If I **will study**, I will pass.

### ✅

> If I **study**, I will pass.

---

### ❌ Type II

> If I **would have** more money, I would travel.

### ✅

> If I **had** more money, I would travel.

---

### ❌ Type III

> If I **would have studied**, I would have passed.

### ✅

> If I **had studied**, I would have passed.

---

### ❌ Mixing Type II and III

> If I studied yesterday, I would have passed.

### Better:

> If I **had studied** yesterday, I **would have passed**.

The time reference must match the conditional type.

---

# 16. How to Identify the Type

Ask yourself:

### Question 1

**Is the condition realistically possible?**

→ **Type I**

> If I study, I will pass.

### Question 2

**Is it imaginary or unlikely now/future?**

→ **Type II**

> If I were rich, I would travel.

### Question 3

**Is it about an unreal past?**

→ **Type III**

> If I had studied, I would have passed.

---

# 17. Quick Comparison

|                 | Type I                   | Type II                     | Type III                               |
| --------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Meaning**     | Real/possible            | Unreal/unlikely             | Unreal past                            |
| **Time**        | Present/future           | Present/future              | Past                                   |
| **If-clause**   | Present simple           | Past simple                 | Past perfect                           |
| **Main clause** | will + V1                | would + V1                  | would have + V3                        |
| **Example**     | If I study, I will pass. | If I studied, I would pass. | If I had studied, I would have passed. |
| **Memory**      | Possible                 | Imaginary                   | Regret/past alternative                |

---

# 18. The Most Important Patterns

Memorize these three:

> **TYPE I:**
> If + **present**, **will + V1**

> **TYPE II:**
> If + **past**, **would + V1**

> **TYPE III:**
> If + **had + V3**, **would have + V3**

### Example with “study”

> **If I study, I will pass.**

> **If I studied, I would pass.**

> **If I had studied, I would have passed.**

This single sequence makes the differences much easier to remember.

# Quick Check

### 1. Complete:

> If I study hard, I ______ pass the exam.

**Answer:** **will**

---

### 2. Identify the type:

> If I were rich, I would travel around the world.

**Answer:** **Type II**

---

### 3. Identify the type:

> If she had studied, she would have passed.

**Answer:** **Type III**

---

### 4. Complete:

> If it ______ tomorrow, we will stay home.

A. rains
B. will rain
C. rained

**Answer: A — rains**

---

### 5. Complete:

> If I ______ you, I would accept the opportunity.

A. am
B. were
C. had been

**Answer: B — were**

---

### 6. Complete:

> If they had arrived earlier, they ______ have caught the bus.

A. will
B. would
C. would have

**Answer: C — would have**

---

### 7. Change to Type II:

> If I have enough money, I will buy a car.

**Answer:**

> If I **had** enough money, I **would buy** a car.

---

### 8. Change to Type III:

> If I study hard, I will pass the exam.

**Answer:**

> If I **had studied** hard, I **would have passed** the exam.

---

### 9. What does “unless” mean?

**Answer:** Approximately **if ... not**.

> Unless you study = If you don't study.

---

### 10. What is the biggest difference between Type II and Type III?

**Answer:** Type II describes an **unreal or unlikely present/future situation**, while Type III describes an **unreal past situation**.
