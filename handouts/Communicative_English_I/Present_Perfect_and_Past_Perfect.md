# Present Perfect and Past Perfect

> **Core idea:** The **Present Perfect** connects a past action to the **present**, while the **Past Perfect** shows that one past action happened **before another past action**.

## 1. Present Perfect

### Form

> **Subject + have/has + past participle (V3)**

| Subject             | Auxiliary | Example                 |
| ------------------- | --------- | ----------------------- |
| I / You / We / They | **have**  | They **have finished**. |
| He / She / It       | **has**   | She **has finished**.   |

### Examples

> I **have read** the book.

> She **has completed** her assignment.

> We **have visited** Bahir Dar.

The Ethiopian freshman module describes the Present Perfect as an action that occurred at an **unspecified time before the present**, often with a connection to the present.

---

## 2. Uses of the Present Perfect

### A. Past action with a present result

> I **have lost** my keys.

**Result:** I cannot find or use them now.

> She **has broken** her phone.

**Result:** The phone is broken now.

---

### B. Life experience

Use it to talk about something someone has or has not experienced.

> I **have visited** Addis Ababa.

> He **has never eaten** sushi.

> **Have** you **ever travelled** abroad?

Common words:

* **ever**
* **never**
* **before**

---

### C. Recent actions

> The class **has just started**.

> She **has just finished** her assignment.

Common words:

* **just**
* **already**
* **yet**

---

### D. An action continuing until now

> We **have lived** here for five years.

> She **has studied** English since 2022.

Use:

* **for** → a period of time
* **since** → the starting point

### Compare

> I have studied English **for five years**.

> I have studied English **since 2021**.

---

## 3. Present Perfect Time Expressions

Common expressions include:

| Expression    | Example                                 |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **already**   | She has already left.                   |
| **just**      | They have just arrived.                 |
| **yet**       | He hasn't finished yet.                 |
| **ever**      | Have you ever been there?               |
| **never**     | I have never seen it.                   |
| **so far**    | We have completed three units so far.   |
| **up to now** | Up to now, we have learned five topics. |
| **since**     | She has lived here since 2020.          |
| **for**       | She has lived here for six years.       |

The Ethiopian module specifically identifies expressions such as **already, never, till now, ever, not yet, up to now, just, and so far** as common with the Present Perfect.

---

# 4. Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

This is one of the most important distinctions.

### Present Perfect

Focuses on the **experience, result, or connection with the present**.

> I **have read** this book.

The exact time is not important or not given.

### Simple Past

Focuses on a **finished action at a definite past time**.

> I **read** this book **last year**.

### Compare

> I **have visited** Hawassa.

> I **visited** Hawassa **last month**.

### Key rule

> **Present Perfect → no definite finished past time**

> **Simple Past → definite past time**

❌ I have visited Hawassa **last year**.

✅ I visited Hawassa **last year**.

The FLEn 1011 module explicitly uses this distinction in its exercises.

---

# 5. Past Perfect

### Form

> **Subject + had + past participle (V3)**

The auxiliary **had** is used with **all subjects**.

| Subject | Form     | Example                |
| ------- | -------- | ---------------------- |
| I       | had + V3 | I **had finished**.    |
| You     | had + V3 | You **had finished**.  |
| He/She  | had + V3 | She **had finished**.  |
| We/They | had + V3 | They **had finished**. |

---

## 6. Use of the Past Perfect

The Past Perfect shows that an action happened **before another action or point in the past**.

### Example

> When I arrived, the class **had started**.

There are two past actions:

1. The class **started**.
2. I **arrived**.

The class started **first**.

### Timeline

```text
PAST
│
├── Class started
│
├── I arrived
│
└── NOW
```

Therefore:

> The class **had started** before I arrived.

---

## 7. Past Perfect with “Before” and “After”

### Before

> The students **had left** before the teacher arrived.

**First:** students left
**Second:** teacher arrived

### After

> After the students **had left**, the teacher arrived.

**First:** students left
**Second:** teacher arrived

### Easy rule

> **Earlier past action → Past Perfect**

> **Later past action → Simple Past**

---

# 8. Present Perfect vs. Past Perfect

This is the key difference:

| Present Perfect                | Past Perfect                          |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| **have/has + V3**              | **had + V3**                          |
| Connects past with **present** | Connects two **past actions**         |
| Time before now                | Time before another past event        |
| I **have finished**.           | I **had finished** before he arrived. |

### Compare

**Present Perfect:**

> I **have eaten** lunch.

Meaning: Lunch is relevant **now**.

**Past Perfect:**

> I **had eaten** lunch before I went to class.

Meaning: Eating happened **before another past event**.

---

# 9. Present Perfect, Past Simple, and Past Perfect

These three are often tested together.

### Present Perfect

> I **have finished** my assignment.

**Connection:** past → present

### Past Simple

> I **finished** my assignment yesterday.

**Connection:** finished past action + definite past time

### Past Perfect

> I **had finished** my assignment before the teacher arrived.

**Connection:** earlier past → later past

### Memory line

> **Present Perfect → past + now**

> **Past Simple → finished past**

> **Past Perfect → past before past**

# 10. Common Signal Words

### Present Perfect

* already
* just
* yet
* ever
* never
* so far
* since
* for
* up to now
* recently

### Past Perfect

* before
* after
* by the time
* already
* when
* until then

> **Warning:** Signal words are useful clues, but the **meaning and timeline** are more important than memorizing lists.

---

# 11. How to Identify the Correct Tense

When you see a sentence, ask:

### Question 1

**Is the action connected to now?**

→ Consider **Present Perfect**.

> She **has finished** her work.

### Question 2

**Is there a definite finished past time?**

→ Use **Simple Past**.

> She **finished** her work yesterday.

### Question 3

**Are there two past actions?**

→ Identify which happened first.

The earlier action → **Past Perfect**

> She **had finished** her work before I arrived.

---

# 12. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Present Perfect + definite past time

> I **have visited** Gondar last year.

### ✅

> I **visited** Gondar last year.

---

### ❌ Wrong auxiliary

> She **have finished** the work.

### ✅

> She **has finished** the work.

---

### ❌ Wrong Past Perfect form

> They **had went** home.

### ✅

> They **had gone** home.

Remember:

> **have / has / had + V3**

---

### ❌ Using Past Perfect without a past reference

> Yesterday I had gone to school.

Usually, simple past is enough:

> Yesterday I **went** to school.

Past Perfect is normally useful when establishing a relationship between **two past points/events**.

---

# 13. Quick Comparison

| Situation                            | Correct tense   | Example                           |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Past action connected to now         | Present Perfect | I **have lost** my phone.         |
| Life experience                      | Present Perfect | I **have visited** Gondar.        |
| Recent action                        | Present Perfect | She **has just arrived**.         |
| Action continuing to now             | Present Perfect | We **have lived** here for years. |
| Finished action at a known past time | Simple Past     | I **visited** Gondar last year.   |
| Earlier of two past actions          | Past Perfect    | I **had left** before he arrived. |

## ⭐ Exam Focus

### Remember the formulas

**Present Perfect**

> **have/has + V3**

**Past Perfect**

> **had + V3**

### The three-timeline rule

> **Present Perfect:**
> **PAST ─────────→ NOW**

> **Past Simple:**
> **PAST ✕**

> **Past Perfect:**
> **EARLIER PAST → LATER PAST**

### Exam shortcut

If you see:

> **since / for / ever / never / already / just / yet**

→ Think **Present Perfect**.

If you see:

> **before / after / by the time + two past events**

→ Check for **Past Perfect**.

But always check the **meaning and timeline**.
