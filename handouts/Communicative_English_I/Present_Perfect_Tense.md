# Present Perfect Tense

> **Core idea:** The **Present Perfect** describes an action or situation that happened before now but is connected in some way to the **present**. The exact past time is usually not stated.

## 1. Form

The Present Perfect is formed with:

> **Subject + have/has + past participle (V3)**

| Subject             | Auxiliary | Example               |
| ------------------- | --------- | --------------------- |
| I / You / We / They | **have**  | We **have finished**. |
| He / She / It       | **has**   | She **has finished**. |

### Positive

> I **have completed** my assignment.
> She **has visited** Gondar.

### Negative

> I **have not completed** my assignment.
> She **has not visited** Gondar.

Short forms:

> have not → **haven't**
> has not → **hasn't**

### Question

> **Have** you finished?
> **Has** she arrived?

---

## 2. What Is the Past Participle?

The past participle is the **third form of a verb (V3)**.

### Regular verbs

Usually:

> work → worked → **worked**
> finish → finished → **finished**
> visit → visited → **visited**

### Irregular verbs

These must be learned individually:

| Base  | Past  | Past Participle |
| ----- | ----- | --------------- |
| go    | went  | **gone**        |
| see   | saw   | **seen**        |
| write | wrote | **written**     |
| eat   | ate   | **eaten**       |
| take  | took  | **taken**       |
| do    | did   | **done**        |
| make  | made  | **made**        |
| read  | read  | **read**        |

> **Important:** Present Perfect always uses **V3**, not the simple past form.

---

# 3. Main Uses of the Present Perfect

The Ethiopian freshman module emphasizes several uses: actions at an unspecified past time, past actions with present results, recently completed actions, ongoing situations, and experiences.

## A. Unspecified Past Time

Use the Present Perfect when an action happened before now, but **the exact time is not important or is not given**.

> I **have read** this book.

> She **has visited** Ethiopia.

We are interested in **whether the action happened**, not exactly when.

### Compare

> I **have read** this book.
> → No specific time.

> I **read** this book **last year**.
> → Specific past time.

---

## B. Past Action With a Present Result

An action happened in the past, but its **result is important now**.

> I **have lost** my keys.

**Present result:** I cannot open the door.

> She **has broken** her phone.

**Present result:** Her phone is broken.

This is one of the examples specifically used in the Ethiopian module.

### Think:

> **Past action → Present result**

---

## C. Recently Completed Actions

The Present Perfect can describe an action that has **just happened or happened recently**.

> Ujulu **has cooked** dinner.

> The class **has just finished**.

The action may have stopped, but it is still relevant to the present.

Common words:

* **just**
* **already**
* **recently**

---

## D. Life Experience

Use the Present Perfect to talk about something someone has experienced **at some time in their life**.

> I **have visited** Hawassa.

> She **has never travelled** abroad.

> **Have** you **ever seen** a population pyramid?

The exact time is not important.

### Common words

> **ever** → at any time

> **never** → at no time

> **before** → previously

---

## E. An Action or Situation Continuing Until Now

The Present Perfect can describe something that **began in the past and continues to the present**.

> I **have lived** here for five years.

> She **has studied** English since 2021.

Use:

### **For** + period of time

> for two years
> for three months
> for a long time

### **Since** + starting point

> since 2021
> since Monday
> since childhood

### Compare

> I have studied English **for five years**.

> I have studied English **since 2021**.

---

# 5. Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

This is one of the **most important exam points**.

### Present Perfect

> I **have visited** Gondar.

The exact time is not stated.

### Simple Past

> I **visited** Gondar **last year**.

The action happened at a **specific, finished time**.

### Key rule

> **Present Perfect → unspecified time / present connection**

> **Simple Past → definite finished past time**

The Ethiopian module gives examples such as “I have read this novel before” followed by a question about **when** it happened, illustrating the distinction between experience and a specific past time.

---

## 6. Definite Past Time: Use Simple Past

Do **not normally** use Present Perfect with a finished, specific past time.

### ❌ Incorrect

> I **have visited** Addis Ababa **last year**.

### ✅ Correct

> I **visited** Addis Ababa **last year**.

Other definite past-time expressions include:

* yesterday
* last week
* last year
* two days ago
* in 2020
* when I was young

### Compare

> I **have seen** that movie.

> I **saw** that movie **last Friday**.

---

# 7. Present Perfect Questions

### Yes/No questions

> **Have** you finished your homework?

> **Has** she arrived?

### WH-questions

> **How many** books have you read?

> **Where** have you been?

> **How long** have you lived here?

### Important distinction

> **Have you visited Gondar?**
> → Experience

> **When did you visit Gondar?**
> → Specific past time

Once we ask for a **specific finished past time**, the answer normally uses the **Simple Past**.

---

# 8. Present Perfect with Already, Yet, and Just

### Already

Used when something happened **before now or earlier than expected**.

> She has **already** finished.

### Just

Used for something that happened **very recently**.

> They have **just** arrived.

### Yet

Commonly used in questions and negative sentences.

> Have you finished **yet**?

> I haven't finished **yet**.

### Quick pattern

> **already** → completed before now

> **just** → very recently

> **yet** → until now, usually questions/negatives

---

# 9. Present Perfect with Ever and Never

### Ever

Used mainly in questions to ask about experience.

> Have you **ever** visited Lalibela?

### Never

Means **not at any time**.

> I have **never** visited Lalibela.

### Compare

> Have you **ever** eaten Ethiopian food?

> No, I have **never** eaten it.

---

# 10. Present Perfect with For and Since

### For = duration

> I have lived here **for five years**.

> She has studied English **for three months**.

### Since = starting point

> I have lived here **since 2021**.

> She has studied English **since September**.

### Easy memory

> **FOR = How long?**

> **SINCE = From when?**

---

# 11. Present Perfect in Academic English

The tense is useful when discussing:

* Research
* Developments
* Changes
* Previous studies
* Experiences
* Results

### Examples

> Researchers **have identified** several important factors.

> Technology **has changed** the way students learn.

> Scholars **have studied** population growth extensively.

The FLEn 1011 reading itself introduces Present Perfect through academic-style examples such as authors having identified issues over the years.

---

# 12. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Using the wrong auxiliary

> She **have finished** her work.

### ✅

> She **has finished** her work.

---

### ❌ Using the simple past instead of V3

> She has **went** home.

### ✅

> She has **gone** home.

---

### ❌ Using Present Perfect with a definite past time

> I have visited Axum **last year**.

### ✅

> I visited Axum **last year**.

---

### ❌ Confusing “for” and “since”

> I have lived here **since five years**.

### ✅

> I have lived here **for five years**.

---

### ❌

> I have lived here **for 2021**.

### ✅

> I have lived here **since 2021**.

---

# 13. Quick Decision Method

When choosing between Present Perfect and Simple Past, ask:

### 1. Is there a specific finished past time?

**Yes** → Simple Past.

> I **visited** Jimma last year.

**No** → Continue.

### 2. Is the action connected to the present?

**Yes** → Present Perfect.

> I **have visited** Jimma.

### 3. Is it a life experience?

**Yes** → Present Perfect.

> I **have never visited** Jimma.

### 4. Is the action continuing until now?

**Yes** → Present Perfect.

> I **have lived** here for five years.
