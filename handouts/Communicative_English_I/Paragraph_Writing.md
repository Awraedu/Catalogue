# Paragraph Writing

> **Core idea:** A **paragraph** is a group of related sentences that develops **one main idea**. A good paragraph is clear, organized, unified, coherent, and sufficiently developed.

## 1. What Is a Paragraph?

A paragraph is a group of sentences that discusses **one main topic or idea**.

A good paragraph should:

* Focus on **one main idea**
* Have a clear organization
* Include relevant supporting information
* Connect ideas logically
* End appropriately

The Ethiopian freshman English materials identify three basic parts of a paragraph: **topic sentence, supporting sentences, and concluding sentence**.

---

## 2. The Three Main Parts

### 1. Topic Sentence

The **topic sentence** introduces the main idea of the paragraph.

It usually contains:

> **Topic + controlling idea**

### Example

> **Regular exercise is important for university students.**

* **Topic:** regular exercise
* **Controlling idea:** is important for university students

The topic sentence tells the reader **what the paragraph will be about**.

---

### 2. Supporting Sentences

Supporting sentences **explain, develop, or provide evidence for** the topic sentence.

They may include:

* Reasons
* Facts
* Examples
* Descriptions
* Explanations
* Details

### Example

> Regular exercise is important for university students. **It improves physical fitness and helps students maintain a healthy weight. It can also reduce stress and improve concentration.**

The bold ideas support the main idea.

---

### 3. Concluding Sentence

The concluding sentence **brings the paragraph to a natural ending**.

It may:

* Restate the main idea
* Summarize the important points
* Give a final thought

### Example

> **Therefore, regular exercise can help university students stay healthy and perform better in their studies.**

---

## 3. Paragraph Structure

A simple structure is:

```text
Topic Sentence
      ↓
Supporting Sentence 1
      ↓
Supporting Sentence 2
      ↓
Supporting Sentence 3
      ↓
Concluding Sentence
```

### Example Paragraph

> **Regular exercise is important for university students.** It helps students maintain physical fitness and a healthy weight. Exercise can also reduce stress and improve concentration. In addition, regular physical activity can improve sleep. **Therefore, exercise is an important part of a healthy student lifestyle.**

---

## 4. Unity

**Unity** means that all sentences in a paragraph support **one main idea**.

### ❌ Poor unity

> Regular exercise is important for students. It improves fitness. **Addis Ababa is the capital of Ethiopia.** Exercise can reduce stress.

The sentence about Addis Ababa does not support the topic.

### ✅ Unified paragraph

> Regular exercise is important for students. It improves fitness. It reduces stress. It can also improve concentration.

Every sentence supports the **same main idea**.

> **Exam tip:** Ask yourself: **“Does every sentence support the topic sentence?”**

---

## 5. Coherence

**Coherence** means that ideas are connected and arranged in a **logical order**.

Use connecting words such as:

* **First**
* **Next**
* **In addition**
* **However**
* **For example**
* **Therefore**
* **As a result**
* **Finally**

### Example

> First, students should identify the topic. **Next**, they should organize their ideas. **Finally**, they can write and revise the paragraph.

The ideas are easy to follow because they are logically connected.

---

## 6. Developing a Paragraph

A paragraph should contain enough information to explain its main idea.

### Weak

> Exercise is useful. It is healthy.

### Better

> Exercise is useful for university students. It improves physical fitness and helps reduce stress. It can also improve concentration and sleep. For these reasons, regular exercise is an important healthy habit.

The second paragraph is better developed because it gives **specific supporting details**.

---

## 7. How to Write a Paragraph

### Step 1: Choose a topic

Example:

> **The importance of reading**

### Step 2: Identify the main idea

> Reading is important for university students.

### Step 3: Brainstorm supporting points

* Improves vocabulary
* Increases knowledge
* Improves concentration
* Helps academic performance

### Step 4: Write the topic sentence

> **Reading is an important habit for university students.**

### Step 5: Add supporting sentences

Explain the points using examples and details.

### Step 6: Add a conclusion

Restate the main idea or give a final thought.

### Step 7: Revise

Check:

* Grammar
* Spelling
* Punctuation
* Organization
* Unity
* Coherence

> **Plan → Write → Support → Conclude → Revise**

---

## 8. Common Types of Paragraphs

### Descriptive Paragraph

Describes a **person, place, object, or situation**.

> Example: describing a university campus.

### Narrative Paragraph

Tells a **story or sequence of events**.

> Example: describing an important experience.

### Expository Paragraph

Explains or gives information about a **topic**.

> Example: explaining how population growth affects cities.

### Compare-and-Contrast Paragraph

Shows **similarities and differences**.

> Example: comparing rural and urban life.

---

## 9. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Too many main ideas

A paragraph should normally focus on **one central idea**.

### ❌ No topic sentence

The reader may not know what the paragraph is about.

### ❌ Unrelated details

Every sentence should contribute to the main idea.

### ❌ Weak development

The writer gives a claim but does not explain it.

### ❌ No conclusion

The paragraph may feel unfinished.

### ❌ Poor connections

Ideas become difficult to follow when linking words are missing.
