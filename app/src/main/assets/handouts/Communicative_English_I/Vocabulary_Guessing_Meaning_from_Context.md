# Vocabulary: Guessing Meaning from Context

> **Core idea:** You do not always need a dictionary to understand an unfamiliar word. **Context clues**—the words and ideas surrounding it—can help you work out its likely meaning.

## 1. What Is Context?

**Context** is the information surrounding a word, sentence, or passage that helps explain its meaning.

### Example

> The athlete was **exhausted** after running for two hours. He could hardly stand.

Even if you do not know **exhausted**, the context tells you that it probably means:

> **very tired**

The second sentence provides a clue.

---

# 2. Why Guess Meaning from Context?

When reading academic texts, you will often encounter unfamiliar vocabulary.

Stopping to look up every unknown word can:

* Slow down your reading
* Interrupt your understanding
* Make reading less efficient

Instead:

> **Read → Look for clues → Guess → Check**

The FLEn 1011 module specifically expects students to develop the ability to **guess the meanings of unfamiliar words using various contextual clues**.

---

# 3. Don't Panic When You See an Unknown Word

You do not need to understand every word immediately.

First ask:

> **Is this word important to understanding the whole sentence?**

If the answer is no, continue reading.

If it is important, look for clues around it.

### Example

> The athlete was **fatigued** after the long race, so he rested for several hours.

Even without knowing *fatigued*, you can infer:

> **fatigued = tired**

The phrase **“so he rested”** provides a useful clue.

---

# 4. Main Types of Context Clues

Important clues include:

1. **Definition**
2. **Example**
3. **Synonym**
4. **Antonym / contrast**
5. **Explanation**
6. **Cause and effect**
7. **Punctuation**
8. **General knowledge**

A British Council resource developed in Ethiopia also identifies context clues such as **knowledge of the world, punctuation, examples, comparison, definition, and contrast**.

---

# 5. Definition Clues

Sometimes the writer directly defines the unfamiliar word.

### Example

> **Stamina**, the ability to continue physical activity for a long time, is important for athletes.

The definition is:

> **Stamina = ability to continue physical activity for a long time**

### Common signals

Look for:

* **means**
* **refers to**
* **is defined as**
* **is**
* **that is**
* **in other words**

### Example

> A **sedentary** lifestyle, **meaning a lifestyle with little physical activity**, can affect health.

**Sedentary = involving little physical activity**

---

# 6. Example Clues

Sometimes examples explain an unfamiliar word.

### Example

> Many forms of **aerobic exercise**, such as **running, swimming, and cycling**, improve cardiovascular fitness.

The examples tell you that aerobic exercise refers to:

> **types of physical activity**

### Common signals

> **for example**

> **for instance**

> **such as**

> **including**

### Strategy

When you see several examples after an unfamiliar word, ask:

> **What do these examples have in common?**

---

# 7. Synonym Clues

A nearby word may have a similar meaning.

### Example

> The athlete was **exhausted**, or **extremely tired**, after the competition.

The synonym is:

> **extremely tired**

Therefore:

> **exhausted = extremely tired**

### Common signals

> **or**

> **in other words**

> **that is**

> **also known as**

---

# 8. Antonym or Contrast Clues

Sometimes the writer gives an opposite idea.

### Example

> Unlike his **energetic** brother, who exercises every morning, Dawit is **inactive** and spends most of his free time sitting.

The contrast helps us understand:

> **energetic ↔ inactive**

### Common signals

> **but**

> **however**

> **unlike**

> **in contrast**

> **whereas**

> **while**

---

# 9. Explanation Clues

The writer may explain an unfamiliar word without giving a direct definition.

### Example

> The patient's condition **deteriorated** during the night. His health became worse and he required immediate treatment.

The second sentence explains:

> **deteriorated = became worse**

### Strategy

Ask:

> **What is happening around the unknown word?**

---

# 10. Cause-and-Effect Clues

The result of an action can reveal the meaning of an unfamiliar word.

### Example

> The athlete was **dehydrated** after exercising for several hours without drinking water. He needed to drink fluids immediately.

You can infer:

> **dehydrated = lacking enough water**

### Pattern

> **Cause → unfamiliar word → effect**

or

> **Unfamiliar word → result**

---

# 11. Punctuation Clues

Punctuation can sometimes introduce an explanation or definition.

Look for:

* Commas
* Dashes
* Parentheses
* Colons
* Brackets

### Example

> The athlete experienced **fatigue — extreme physical tiredness —** after the race.

The dashes give the meaning.

> **fatigue = extreme physical tiredness**

### Another example

> A balanced diet contains essential **nutrients (substances needed for healthy growth and development).**

The parentheses provide the definition.

---

# 12. Comparison Clues

A word may be understood by comparing it with something familiar.

### Example

> Unlike a **sprint**, which requires maximum speed for a short distance, a marathon requires **endurance** over a long distance.

The contrast between sprinting and marathon running helps explain:

> **endurance = ability to continue for a long time**

---

# 13. General Knowledge

Sometimes your knowledge of the world helps you guess.

### Example

> The runner was **shivering** as he waited outside in the freezing weather.

You may infer:

> **shivering = shaking because of cold**

You already know that people often shake when they are very cold.

This is sometimes called using **background knowledge**.

---

# 14. Use More Than One Clue

The strongest guesses often come from **several clues together**.

### Example

> After exercising under the hot sun for several hours without drinking water, the athlete became **dehydrated**. He was extremely thirsty and needed fluids.

Clues:

* Hot weather
* Long exercise
* No water
* Extremely thirsty
* Needed fluids

Therefore:

> **dehydrated = lacking sufficient water**

---

# 15. A Step-by-Step Strategy

Use this method when you meet an unfamiliar word.

### Step 1 — Read the whole sentence

Don't focus only on the unknown word.

### Step 2 — Read the surrounding sentences

The clue may appear before or after it.

### Step 3 — Look for signal words

Check for:

> **for example, such as, however, unlike, means, in other words**

### Step 4 — Identify the clue type

Is it:

> Definition? Example? Contrast? Explanation? Cause/effect?

### Step 5 — Make a reasonable guess

You do not need the exact dictionary definition.

### Step 6 — Replace the word

Put your guessed meaning into the sentence.

### Step 7 — Check the meaning

Does the sentence still make sense?

> **Context → Clue → Guess → Check**

---

# 16. Don't Guess Too Quickly

A single sentence may not provide enough information.

### Weak approach

> I don't know the word → immediately guess.

### Better approach

> Read sentence → read surrounding sentences → identify clues → make a guess.

### Example

> The patient's symptoms were **persistent**. They continued for several weeks despite treatment.

Now the context gives:

> **persistent = continuing for a long time**

---

# 17. Guess the Part of Speech

Grammar can also help.

Ask:

> Is the unknown word a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb?

### Example

> Regular exercise is **beneficial** to health.

The word follows **is** and describes exercise.

Therefore it is probably an:

> **adjective**

The context suggests:

> **beneficial = helpful**

### Useful clues

| Form      | Likely role |
| --------- | ----------- |
| **-tion** | noun        |
| **-ness** | noun        |
| **-ment** | noun        |
| **-ful**  | adjective   |
| **-less** | adjective   |
| **-ly**   | adverb      |
| **-ize**  | verb        |

These endings are not perfect rules, but they can help.

---

# 18. Guess the Meaning, Not Every Detail

Sometimes you only need the **general meaning**.

### Example

> The athlete consumed a **substantial** amount of water after the race.

You may not know the exact dictionary definition of *substantial*.

From the context, you can understand:

> **a large amount**

That's enough to understand the sentence.

> **Context guessing is about useful understanding, not perfect dictionary definitions.**

The FLEn 1011 Unit 2 vocabulary activity is connected to the **Health and Fitness** reading.

### Example

> Regular exercise can **enhance** physical fitness. It can make the body stronger and improve endurance.

What does **enhance** mean?

The surrounding information says:

> make the body stronger + improve endurance

Therefore:

> **enhance = improve**

# 20. Example: Context Clue Practice

### Sentence 1

> The athlete was **exhausted** after the marathon and immediately went to sleep.

**Guess:**

> exhausted = **very tired**

**Clue:** He immediately went to sleep.

---

### Sentence 2

> Unlike **sedentary** people, active people exercise regularly.

**Guess:**

> sedentary = **not physically active**

**Clue:** Contrast with active.

---

### Sentence 3

> **Hydration**, the process of providing the body with enough water, is important during exercise.

**Guess:**

> hydration = **providing/getting enough water**

**Clue:** Direct definition.

---

### Sentence 4

> Many aerobic activities, **such as running, cycling, and swimming**, improve cardiovascular fitness.

**Guess:**

> aerobic activities = **types of physical exercise**

**Clue:** Examples.

---

# 21. Context vs Dictionary

A dictionary is useful, but it should not always be your first step.

### Use context when:

* The general meaning is clear.
* You are reading quickly.
* The word is not essential.
* The surrounding text provides clues.

### Use a dictionary when:

* The word is essential.
* Your guess is uncertain.
* You need the exact meaning.
* You need pronunciation or usage.
* You want to learn the word permanently.

> **Best strategy: Guess first → verify when necessary.**

# 22. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Looking up every unfamiliar word

This interrupts reading.

### ✅ First try to infer the meaning.

---

### ❌ Using only one word as a clue

### ✅ Consider the whole sentence and surrounding text.

---

### ❌ Assuming the first possible meaning is correct

### ✅ Test your guess in the sentence.

---

### ❌ Trying to find the exact dictionary definition

### ✅ First identify the general meaning needed to understand the passage.

---

### ❌ Ignoring contrast words

Words such as **however, unlike, but, and in contrast** can completely change the meaning.

### ✅ Pay attention to signal words.

## ⭐ Exam Focus

For FLEn 1011, you should be able to **guess the meaning of unfamiliar vocabulary using contextual clues**.

### Memorize these major clue types:

| Clue                     | What to look for                    |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| **Definition**           | *means, refers to, is defined as*   |
| **Example**              | *for example, such as, including*   |
| **Synonym**              | Similar word nearby                 |
| **Contrast**             | *but, however, unlike, in contrast* |
| **Explanation**          | A sentence explains the idea        |
| **Cause/effect**         | What happens before/after           |
| **Punctuation**          | Commas, dashes, parentheses         |
| **Background knowledge** | What you already know               |

### Fast exam method

> **1. Read around the word.**
> **2. Find the clue.**
> **3. Identify the clue type.**
> **4. Guess the meaning.**
> **5. Put it back into the sentence.**

# Quick Check

### 1. What is context?

**Answer:** The words, sentences, and ideas surrounding a word that help explain its meaning.

### 2. What does “for example” usually signal?

**Answer:** An example clue.

### 3. What does “unlike” usually signal?

**Answer:** A contrast.

### 4. What type of clue is this?

> “A sedentary lifestyle, meaning a lifestyle with little physical activity, can affect health.”

**Answer:** **Definition**

### 5. Guess the meaning:

> “The athlete was exhausted after the race and could barely walk.”

**Answer:** **Very tired**

### 6. Guess the meaning:

> “Unlike active students, sedentary students spend much of their time sitting.”

**Answer:** **Not physically active**

### 7. Guess the meaning:

> “Many aerobic exercises, such as swimming and cycling, improve fitness.”

**Answer:** **Types of physical exercise**

### 8. What should you do if your first guess does not make sense?

**Answer:** Re-read the surrounding context and make a new guess or check a dictionary.

### 9. Do you always need the exact dictionary definition?

**Answer:** No. A reasonable meaning that allows you to understand the sentence is often enough.

### 10. Complete:

> **Context → ______ → Guess → Check**

**Answer:** **Clue**
