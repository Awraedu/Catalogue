# Reviewing and Improving Lecture Notes

> **Core idea:** Good lecture notes should not simply be written and forgotten. **Reviewing** helps you understand and remember the lesson, while **improving** your notes makes them clearer, more organized, and more useful for assignments and exams.

## 1. Why Review Lecture Notes?

Reviewing notes helps you:

* Remember important information
* Identify gaps in your understanding
* Correct mistakes
* Organize ideas
* Clarify difficult points
* Prepare for examinations
* Turn short lecture notes into useful study material

The Ethiopian FLEn 1011 module specifically encourages students to make lecture notes and use them when **revising their courses**.

> **Lecture notes are a starting point, not the finished product.**

---

# 2. When Should You Review?

The best time to review lecture notes is **soon after the lecture**, while the information is still fresh.

### A useful routine

**After the lecture:**

> **Read → Complete → Organize → Clarify → Summarize → Review**

### Why review early?

You are more likely to remember:

* What the lecturer explained
* The meaning of abbreviations
* Examples that were discussed
* Important points that you did not have time to write

---

# 3. First: Read Through Your Notes

Read your notes from beginning to end.

Ask:

> Do the notes make sense?

> Can I understand what I wrote?

> Are any important ideas missing?

> Are there words or abbreviations I no longer understand?

If something is unclear, try to complete it while you still remember the lecture.

---

# 4. Complete Missing Information

During a fast lecture, you may not have enough time to write everything.

You might write:

> Population density = pop. ÷ area

Later, improve it to:

> **Population density = total population ÷ land area**

Use your memory, textbook, course materials, or reliable sources to fill important gaps.

> **Do not try to rewrite the entire lecture. Complete only what is necessary.**

---

# 5. Organize Your Notes

Well-organized notes are much easier to study.

Use:

* Headings
* Subheadings
* Bullet points
* Numbered lists
* Tables
* Short definitions
* Examples
* Key terms

### Example

Instead of:

> population density number people land area high places cities low rural...

Write:

### Population Density

> **Definition:** Number of people living per unit of land area.

**Formula:**

> Population density = Population ÷ Land area

**High density:** Cities and densely settled areas

**Low density:** Sparsely populated areas

---

# 6. Add Headings and Subheadings

Headings help you see the structure of the lecture.

### Poor organization

> Population growth birth rate death rate migration urbanization density distribution...

### Better organization

> **Population**
>
> **1. Population Growth**
>
> * Birth rate
> * Death rate
>
> **2. Population Distribution**
>
> * Where people live
>
> **3. Population Density**
>
> * People per unit of land
>
> **4. Migration**
>
> * Movement of people

> **Good headings turn a collection of notes into a study guide.**

---

# 7. Highlight Key Information

Do not highlight everything.

Highlight only:

* Definitions
* Main ideas
* Important terms
* Formulas
* Dates
* Important examples
* Key conclusions

### Example

> **Population density** = number of people per unit of land.

The important term and definition should stand out.

> **If everything is highlighted, nothing is highlighted.**

---

# 8. Clarify Abbreviations and Symbols

Students often use abbreviations to write faster.

For example:

> pop. = population
> govt. = government
> e.g. = for example
> ↑ = increase
> ↓ = decrease
> → = leads to

After the lecture, make sure the abbreviations are still clear.

### Example

During lecture:

> ↑ pop. → ↑ demand

After review:

> **Population increases → demand for services increases.**

---

# 9. Add Important Details

Your lecturer may explain an idea verbally without writing everything on the board.

Add useful:

* Examples
* Explanations
* Definitions
* Causes
* Effects
* Comparisons
* Formulas

### Example

Original note:

> **Migration = movement of people**

Improved:

> **Migration:** movement of people from one place to another.
>
> **Reasons:** employment, education, conflict, environmental conditions.

The second version is more useful for revision.

---

# 10. Identify Main Ideas

Not every sentence in a lecture is equally important.

Separate:

> **Main idea → Supporting details → Examples**

### Example

**Main idea:**

> Population growth increases demand for public services.

**Details:**

* More schools may be needed.
* More healthcare facilities may be required.
* More housing may be needed.

**Example:**

> Rapid urban growth can increase pressure on city infrastructure.

This structure makes revision faster.

---

# 11. Mark What You Do Not Understand

Use a simple symbol such as:

> **? = unclear**

Example:

> Population transition → **?**

Then investigate the question later.

You can:

* Check the textbook
* Review your course materials
* Ask the lecturer
* Discuss it with classmates
* Look for a reliable explanation

> **Don't leave a question mark unexplained before an exam.**

---

# 12. Add Your Own Questions

Turn notes into questions.

### Notes

> **Population density:** population ÷ land area.

### Study question

> **What is the formula for population density?**

Answer:

> **Population ÷ land area**

This transforms passive notes into **active revision material**.

---

# 13. Create a Short Summary

After reviewing a section, summarize it in your own words.

### Lecture notes

> Population density = number of people per unit area. High density occurs where many people live in a small area. Low density occurs where fewer people live over a large area.

### Summary

> **Population density measures how concentrated people are in an area.**

A good summary should be:

* Short
* Accurate
* Written in your own words
* Focused on the main idea

---

# 14. Use the Cornell-Style Approach

A simple three-part structure can make notes easier to review:

| Questions / Keywords        | Notes                     |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| What is population density? | People per unit of land   |
| Formula?                    | Population ÷ area         |
| High density?               | Many people in small area |
| Low density?                | Few people in large area  |

### Bottom: Summary

> Population density measures the concentration of people in a particular area.

This format makes your notes useful for **self-testing**.

---

# 15. Compare Notes With Course Materials

Your lecture notes should work together with your textbook or module.

Check whether you missed:

* Definitions
* Important concepts
* Examples
* Formulas
* Dates
* Key explanations

But avoid copying the entire textbook into your notebook.

> **Lecture notes = what the lecturer emphasizes.**
> **Textbook/module = additional explanation and reference.**

---

# 16. Improve Notes Before an Exam

Before an examination, turn your notes into a compact revision sheet.

Include:

### Key terms

> Population density
> Population distribution
> Migration

### Definitions

> Short and accurate meanings.

### Formulas

> Population density = Population ÷ Area

### Main ideas

> The most important concepts.

### Comparisons

> High vs. low density.

### Possible questions

> Questions you expect to see in the exam.

---

# 17. The Review Cycle

A powerful study cycle is:

> **Lecture → Review → Improve → Test → Review again**

### 1. Lecture

Take short, active notes.

### 2. Review

Read them soon after class.

### 3. Improve

Fill gaps and organize information.

### 4. Test

Cover the answers and try to recall the information.

### 5. Review again

Return to difficult areas.

This is more effective than simply rereading the same notes repeatedly.

---

# 18. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Mistake 1: Never reviewing notes

Notes become difficult to understand later.

### ❌ Mistake 2: Copying everything

You spend your time writing instead of listening.

### ❌ Mistake 3: Highlighting everything

Important information no longer stands out.

### ❌ Mistake 4: Keeping unclear abbreviations

You may forget what they mean.

### ❌ Mistake 5: Never adding missing information

Important explanations may be lost.

### ❌ Mistake 6: Rewriting everything

Reviewing does not mean making a beautiful copy of the lecture.

### ❌ Mistake 7: Only rereading

Rereading feels easy but does not always show whether you actually remember the material.

> **Better:** Read → Close the notes → Recall → Check.

---

# 19. Active Review

Instead of asking:

> “Does this look familiar?”

Ask:

> **“Can I explain this without looking?”**

### Example

Close your notes and answer:

> What is population density?

If you can explain it correctly, you understand it.

If you cannot:

> Reopen → Review → Close → Try again.

This is **active recall**.

---

# 20. A 10-Minute Post-Lecture Routine

You do not need hours immediately after every lecture.

### Minutes 1–3

**Read your notes.**

### Minutes 4–5

**Complete missing information.**

### Minutes 6–7

**Organize headings and key terms.**

### Minutes 8–9

**Write a short summary.**

### Minute 10

**Write 2–3 questions for later review.**

> **10 minutes of focused review can make later studying much easier.**

## Quick Check

### 1. Why should you review lecture notes soon after class?

**Answer:** Because the lecture is still fresh in your memory, making it easier to complete and clarify your notes.

### 2. Should you copy the entire lecture into your notes?

**Answer:** No. Record the main ideas, important details, examples, and key information.

### 3. What should you do with unclear information?

**Answer:** Mark it, investigate it, and clarify it before it becomes a knowledge gap.

### 4. Why are headings useful?

**Answer:** They organize information and make important sections easier to find.

### 5. What should you highlight?

**Answer:** Key terms, definitions, formulas, important facts, examples, and conclusions.

### 6. What is active recall?

**Answer:** Trying to remember or explain information **without looking at your notes**.

### 7. Which is better?

A. Read the same notes repeatedly.
B. Read, close the notes, recall, and check.

**Answer: B**

### 8. Complete the process:

> Lecture → Review → ______ → Test

**Answer:** **Improve**
