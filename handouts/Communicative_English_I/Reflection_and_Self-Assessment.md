# Reflection and Self-Assessment

> **Core idea:** **Reflection** means thinking carefully about what you learned, how you learned it, and what you need to improve. **Self-assessment** means judging your own progress and identifying your strengths and weaknesses.

## 1. What Is Reflection?

**Reflection** is the process of looking back at your learning experience and thinking about:

* What you learned
* What you understood well
* What was difficult
* What strategies helped you
* What you need to improve
* How you can learn more effectively

The Ethiopian FLEn 1011 course uses reflection as part of its learning approach, encouraging students to think about the activities they perform in each unit.

### Example

Instead of simply saying:

> “I studied reading.”

A useful reflection is:

> “I learned how to identify the main idea and supporting details. I still need to improve my ability to understand unfamiliar vocabulary from context.”

---

# 2. What Is Self-Assessment?

**Self-assessment** is evaluating your own knowledge, skills, and progress.

You ask yourself:

> **Can I do this successfully?**

For example:

| Skill                                    | My level       |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| I can identify the main idea.            | ✓              |
| I can find specific information.         | ✓              |
| I can take effective notes.              | ✓              |
| I can use the Present Perfect correctly. | Needs practice |
| I can write a clear paragraph.           | ✓              |

Self-assessment helps you recognize what you **can already do** and what requires more practice.

---

# 3. Reflection vs. Self-Assessment

These ideas are related but different.

| Reflection                | Self-Assessment                 |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Think about your learning | Judge your level of achievement |
| Focuses on experience     | Focuses on performance          |
| What did I learn?         | Can I do it?                    |
| What was difficult?       | How well can I do it?           |
| How can I improve?        | What still needs practice?      |

### Easy memory

> **Reflection = Think about learning**

> **Self-assessment = Evaluate learning**

---

# 4. Why Is Reflection Important?

Reflection helps students:

* Understand their learning process
* Identify strengths
* Recognize weaknesses
* Improve study strategies
* Become more independent learners
* Set realistic goals
* Learn from mistakes
* Monitor their progress

The FLEn 1011 module is designed to develop students' ability to learn independently, and reflection is integrated into the activities throughout the course.

---

# 5. Why Is Self-Assessment Important?

Self-assessment helps you answer:

> **What can I do now that I could not do before?**

It can help you:

* Measure progress
* Identify gaps in knowledge
* Prepare for examinations
* Decide what to revise
* Set learning goals
* Take responsibility for your learning

### Example

Before studying:

> “I cannot identify the main idea of a paragraph.”

After practice:

> “I can usually identify the main idea, but I need more practice with implied main ideas.”

That is useful self-assessment.

---

# 6. Questions for Reflection

After completing a lesson, ask:

### What did I learn?

> What new knowledge or skill did I gain?

### What did I understand well?

> Which parts were easy for me?

### What was difficult?

> Which concepts or activities challenged me?

### What helped me learn?

> Which strategy worked best?

### What mistakes did I make?

> What can I learn from those mistakes?

### What should I improve?

> Which skill needs more practice?

### What will I do next?

> What specific action will I take to improve?

---

# 7. A Simple Reflection Model

Use:

> **L → D → S → A**

### **L — Learned**

What did I learn?

### **D — Difficult**

What was difficult?

### **S — Strategy**

What helped me?

### **A — Action**

What will I do next?

### Example

> **Learned:** I learned how to distinguish the Present Simple from the Past Simple.
>
> **Difficult:** I sometimes confuse irregular past verbs.
>
> **Strategy:** Practicing example sentences helped me.
>
> **Action:** I will review common irregular verbs and use them in sentences.

---

# 8. Self-Assessment Checklist

A simple checklist can measure your achievement.

### Study Skills

* [ ] I can prepare effectively for a lecture.
* [ ] I can listen for main ideas and important details.
* [ ] I can take useful lecture notes.
* [ ] I can review and improve my notes.
* [ ] I can make notes from reading.

### Reading

* [ ] I can identify the topic.
* [ ] I can identify the main idea.
* [ ] I can find supporting details.
* [ ] I can skim for general information.
* [ ] I can scan for specific information.
* [ ] I can use context clues.

### Grammar

* [ ] I can use the Present Perfect.
* [ ] I can distinguish Present Simple from Past Simple.
* [ ] I can use conditionals correctly.
* [ ] I can distinguish active and passive voice.

### Writing

* [ ] I can write a clear paragraph.
* [ ] I can organize my ideas logically.
* [ ] I can support a main idea with details.

---

# 9. Rating Your Progress

You can use a simple three-level system:

| Level                | Meaning                                 |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 🟢 **Can do**        | I can do it independently.              |
| 🟡 **Almost there**  | I understand it but need more practice. |
| 🔴 **Need practice** | I still need help or more study.        |

### Example

> **Identify main ideas:** 🟢 Can do
> **Use Present Perfect:** 🟡 Almost there
> **Write a paragraph:** 🔴 Need practice

This makes your weaknesses easy to identify.

---

# 10. Setting Learning Goals

A good reflection should lead to **action**.

Instead of:

> “I need to improve English.”

Set a specific goal:

> “I will practice five Present Perfect sentences every day.”

Instead of:

> “My reading is weak.”

Try:

> “I will read one short English passage each day and identify its main idea and two supporting details.”

### Good goals are:

* Specific
* Realistic
* Measurable
* Time-focused

---

# 11. Learning From Mistakes

Mistakes are useful when you analyze them.

### Example

You write:

> ❌ She have finished her assignment.

Correct:

> ✅ She **has finished** her assignment.

Reflection:

> “I confused **have** and **has**. I need to remember that **he/she/it → has**.”

The goal is not simply to correct the mistake but to understand **why it happened**.

---

# 12. Reflection After Reading

After reading a text, ask:

1. What was the topic?
2. What was the main idea?
3. What important details did I learn?
4. Which words were unfamiliar?
5. Could I understand their meanings from context?
6. What reading strategy did I use?
7. What was difficult?
8. What should I improve?

This connects reflection directly to the reading and study skills emphasized in FLEn 1011.

---

# 13. Reflection After a Lecture

After a lecture, ask:

> **What was the main topic?**

> **What were the three most important ideas?**

> **What details did I record?**

> **What did I not understand?**

> **Are my notes organized?**

> **What should I review?**

A useful routine is:

> **Review → Identify gaps → Clarify → Practice**

---

# 14. Effective vs. Weak Reflection

### Weak

> “The lesson was good.”

This gives little information.

### Better

> “I understood the main ideas, but I found some vocabulary difficult.”

### Strong

> “I understood the main ideas and could identify supporting details. However, I struggled with unfamiliar academic vocabulary. I will use context clues and review new words after reading.”

> **Good reflection is specific, honest, and action-oriented.**

---

# 15. A Complete Self-Assessment Example

### Skill

> **I can identify the main idea of a paragraph.**

### Assessment

> 🟢 **Can do**

### Evidence

> I can usually identify the main idea without looking at the answer.

### Weakness

> I sometimes confuse a supporting detail with the main idea.

### Action

> I will practice identifying the topic, main idea, and supporting details in five paragraphs.

This is much more useful than simply writing:

> “I am good at reading.”

## ⭐ Exam Focus

Remember these definitions:

> **Reflection:** Thinking critically about your learning experience.

> **Self-assessment:** Evaluating your own knowledge, skills, and progress.

### Key reflection questions

**What did I learn?**

**What was difficult?**

**What did I do well?**

**What mistakes did I make?**

**What do I need to improve?**

**What will I do next?**

### Key principle

> **Reflection without action is incomplete.**

A good reflection should lead to a **specific improvement plan**.

## Quick Check

### 1. What is reflection?

**Answer:** Thinking carefully about your learning experience, progress, difficulties, and ways to improve.

### 2. What is self-assessment?

**Answer:** Evaluating your own knowledge, skills, and progress.

### 3. What is the main difference between reflection and self-assessment?

**Answer:** Reflection focuses on **thinking about learning**, while self-assessment focuses on **evaluating achievement**.

### 4. Why is reflection important?

**Answer:** It helps identify strengths, weaknesses, effective strategies, and areas for improvement.

### 5. What should you do after identifying a weakness?

**Answer:** Create a specific plan to improve it.

### 6. Which is a better reflection?

A. “English is difficult.”
B. “I can identify main ideas, but I need more practice with implied main ideas.”

**Answer: B**

### 7. What does 🟡 “Almost there” mean?

**Answer:** You understand the skill but need more practice.

### 8. Complete:

> Reflection → Identify weakness → ______

**Answer:** **Take action / make an improvement plan.**
