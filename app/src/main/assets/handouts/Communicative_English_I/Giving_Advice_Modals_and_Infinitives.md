# Giving Advice: Modals and Infinitives

> **Core idea:** We use **modal verbs** and **infinitives** to give advice, make recommendations, and suggest what someone should or should not do.

## 1. Giving Advice with Modal Verbs

### Should / Shouldn't

**Should** is the most common way to give advice.

**Structure:**

> **Subject + should + base verb**

* You **should study** regularly.
* She **should ask** the teacher for help.
* You **shouldn't waste** your time.

**Shouldn't** means that something is not advisable.

---

## 2. Ought to

**Ought to** has a meaning similar to **should**.

**Structure:**

> **Subject + ought to + base verb**

* You **ought to review** your notes.
* Students **ought to attend** lectures regularly.
* You **ought not to ignore** the instructions.

> In everyday English, **should** is generally more common than **ought to**.

---

## 3. Had Better

**Had better** gives **strong advice**, often because there may be a negative consequence.

**Structure:**

> **Subject + had better + base verb**

* You **had better study** for the exam.
* We **had better leave** early.
* You **had better not be** late.

⚠️ Do not use **to** after *had better*.

❌ You had better **to study**.
✅ You had better **study**.

---

## 4. Advice with Infinitives

An **infinitive** is usually:

> **to + base verb**

Examples:

* to study
* to practice
* to review
* to exercise
* to improve

We can use infinitives after certain expressions when giving advice.

### It is a good idea to...

> It is a good idea **to review your notes**.

### It is important to...

> It is important **to attend lectures**.

### I advise you to...

> I advise you **to start early**.

### I recommend that you...

> I recommend that you **practice regularly**.

---

## 5. Common Advice Expressions

| Expression                  | Example                                 |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **You should...**           | You should study regularly.             |
| **You shouldn't...**        | You shouldn't stay up too late.         |
| **You ought to...**         | You ought to ask for help.              |
| **You had better...**       | You had better leave now.               |
| **I advise you to...**      | I advise you to practice.               |
| **I recommend that you...** | I recommend that you review the lesson. |
| **It is a good idea to...** | It is a good idea to take notes.        |
| **It is important to...**   | It is important to prepare.             |

---

## 6. Strong vs. Weak Advice

Different expressions show different levels of strength.

**Mild advice:**

> You **could** talk to your teacher.

**Normal advice:**

> You **should** talk to your teacher.

**Strong advice:**

> You **had better** talk to your teacher.

### Compare

> You **could** study tonight.
> → One possible choice.

> You **should** study tonight.
> → Good advice.

> You **had better** study tonight.
> → Strong advice; there may be consequences.

---

## 7. Asking for Advice

Common questions include:

* **What should I do?**
* **What do you think I should do?**
* **What would you recommend?**
* **Do you think I should...?**
* **What would you advise me to do?**

### Example

**A:** I have an exam tomorrow. What should I do?
**B:** You should review the main topics and get enough sleep.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

### After should → base verb

❌ You should **to study**.
✅ You should **study**.

### After ought → to + base verb

❌ You ought **study**.
✅ You ought **to study**.

### After had better → base verb

❌ You had better **to leave**.
✅ You had better **leave**.

### After advise + person → to + verb

❌ I advise you **study**.
✅ I advise you **to study**.

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

Remember these patterns:

> **should + base verb**
> *You should study.*

> **ought to + base verb**
> *You ought to study.*

> **had better + base verb**
> *You had better study.*

> **advise + object + to-infinitive**
> *I advise you to study.*

> **It is important + to-infinitive**
> *It is important to study.*

### Quick Memory Trick

**Modal → no "to"**

> should **study**
> had better **study**

**Ought → "to"**

> ought **to study**

---

## Quick Check

1. You ______ study for the exam.
   **Answer:** should

2. You ought ______ review your notes.
   **Answer:** to

3. You had better ______ early.
   **Answer:** leave

4. I advise you ______ more carefully.
   **Answer:** to read

5. Which is stronger: **should** or **had better**?
   **Answer:** Had better

---

> **💡 Remember:**
> **Should = advice**
> **Ought to = advice**
> **Had better = strong advice**
> **To + verb = infinitive**
