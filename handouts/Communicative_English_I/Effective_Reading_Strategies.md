# Effective Reading Strategies

> **Core idea:** Effective reading means using the **right strategy for your purpose** so that you can understand, remember, and use information efficiently.

## 1. Set a Purpose

Before reading, ask:

> **Why am I reading this?**

Your purpose determines how you should read.

* **Study** → understand and remember ideas
* **Find information** → locate specific facts
* **Understand a topic** → read for main ideas and details
* **Review** → quickly recall important information
* **Enjoyment** → focus on the overall experience

---

## 2. Preview the Text

Before reading carefully, quickly examine:

* Title
* Headings and subheadings
* Introduction
* Pictures, charts, and diagrams
* Bold or italic words
* Summary or conclusion
* Questions at the end

This gives you an idea of **what the text is about**.

---

## 3. Skimming

**Skimming** means reading quickly to get the **main idea or general meaning**.

Focus on:

* Title
* Headings
* First and last paragraphs
* Topic sentences
* Key words

> **Question answered:** *What is this text mainly about?*

**Example:** Skim a chapter to decide whether it contains information you need for an assignment.

---

## 4. Scanning

**Scanning** means reading quickly to find **specific information**.

Look for:

* Names
* Dates
* Numbers
* Definitions
* Specific terms
* Answers to questions

> **Question answered:** *Where is the specific information I need?*

**Example:** Scan a textbook to find the year an event occurred.

---

## 5. Reading for Main Ideas and Details

The **main idea** is the most important point of a paragraph or text.

**Supporting details** explain, prove, or develop the main idea.

### Example

> Regular exercise improves students' physical and mental health. It can increase energy, improve concentration, and reduce stress.

**Main idea:** Exercise benefits students' health.

**Details:** It can increase energy, improve concentration, and reduce stress.

### Tip

Look for the **topic sentence**, often found near the beginning of a paragraph.

---

## 6. Reading for Understanding

When reading difficult material:

### **Predict**

Think about what the text may discuss.

### **Question**

Ask questions about the information.

### **Connect**

Relate new information to what you already know.

### **Clarify**

Look up unfamiliar words or confusing ideas.

### **Summarize**

Explain the main idea using your own words.

A useful cycle is:

> **Preview → Read → Question → Clarify → Summarize → Review**

---

## 7. Use Context Clues

You can often understand an unfamiliar word from the words and sentences around it.

### Common context clues

* **Definition:** gives the meaning directly
* **Example:** provides an example
* **Contrast:** shows an opposite idea
* **Cause/effect:** explains a relationship

### Example

> The area is **arid**, meaning it receives very little rainfall.

The phrase *receives very little rainfall* helps you understand **arid**.

---

## 8. Take Useful Notes

Do not copy everything.

Record:

* Main ideas
* Key terms
* Important facts
* Definitions
* Examples
* Causes and effects
* Important dates or numbers
* Questions you do not understand

Use short phrases, symbols, and your own words.

> **Good notes capture ideas, not every sentence.**

---

## 9. Active Reading

**Active reading** means interacting with the text instead of simply looking at the words.

While reading:

* Underline important ideas
* Write short notes
* Ask questions
* Identify key terms
* Make connections
* Predict what comes next
* Summarize sections

This improves **understanding and memory**.

---

## 10. Choose the Right Strategy

| Purpose                        | Best Strategy                 |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Get the general idea           | **Skimming**                  |
| Find a specific fact           | **Scanning**                  |
| Study for an exam              | **Detailed/active reading**   |
| Understand an argument         | **Critical reading**          |
| Remember important information | **Note-taking + summarizing** |
| Understand an unfamiliar word  | **Context clues**             |

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

Remember the difference:

> **Skimming → Main idea**
> **Scanning → Specific information**

Also remember:

> **Effective reading = Purpose + Strategy + Active Thinking**

When reading a study text, don't just ask **“What does this say?”** Ask **“What is the main idea, what supports it, and why is it important?”**

---

## Quick Check

1. What should you do before reading a text carefully?
   **Answer:** Set a purpose and preview the text.

2. What is skimming used for?
   **Answer:** Finding the main idea or general meaning.

3. What is scanning used for?
   **Answer:** Finding specific information.

4. What are supporting details?
   **Answer:** Information that explains or supports the main idea.

5. What is active reading?
   **Answer:** Reading while actively thinking, questioning, connecting, and summarizing the information.

---

> **💡 Remember:**
> **Skim for the big picture. Scan for the exact answer. Read actively for understanding.**
