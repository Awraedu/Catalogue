# Contrasting Verb Tenses

> **Core idea:** **Contrasting verb tenses** means choosing between different verb tenses to show **when an action happens** and how actions are related in time.

## 1. Why Do We Contrast Tenses?

Different tenses help us show whether an action is:

* happening **now**
* happening **regularly**
* completed **in the past**
* continuing from the past to the present
* happening before or after another event
* expected to happen in the future

---

## 2. Present Simple vs. Present Continuous

### Present Simple

Used for **habits, routines, facts, and general truths**.

> **I study English every day.**

### Present Continuous

Used for an action **happening now or around the present time**.

> **I am studying English now.**

| Present Simple                 | Present Continuous             |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| I study every day.             | I am studying now.             |
| She works at a bank.           | She is working now.            |
| They play football on Sundays. | They are playing football now. |

**Key clues:**

* Present Simple → *usually, always, every day, often*
* Present Continuous → *now, at the moment, currently*

---

## 3. Past Simple vs. Present Perfect

### Past Simple

Used for a **completed action at a definite time in the past**.

> I visited Addis Ababa **last year**.

### Present Perfect

Used for a past action connected to the **present**, often when the exact time is not important.

> I have visited Addis Ababa **several times**.

| Past Simple                      | Present Perfect               |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| I saw the movie yesterday.       | I have seen the movie before. |
| She finished her work at 5:00.   | She has finished her work.    |
| They went to Hawassa last month. | They have been to Hawassa.    |

⚠️ **Important:** Do not normally use the present perfect with a finished past-time expression.

❌ I have seen him yesterday.
✅ I saw him yesterday.

---

## 4. Past Simple vs. Past Continuous

### Past Simple

Shows a **completed past action**.

> I studied last night.

### Past Continuous

Shows an action that **was in progress at a particular time in the past**.

> I was studying at 8 p.m.

They are often used together:

> **I was studying when my friend called.**

* **was studying** → action in progress
* **called** → shorter action that interrupted it

---

## 5. Present Perfect vs. Present Perfect Continuous

### Present Perfect

Focuses mainly on the **result or completion**.

> She has written three pages.

### Present Perfect Continuous

Focuses on the **duration or ongoing activity**.

> She has been writing for two hours.

| Present Perfect          | Present Perfect Continuous         |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Focus on result          | Focus on activity/duration         |
| I have read the book.    | I have been reading for two hours. |
| He has cleaned the room. | He has been cleaning the room.     |

**Common clues:**

* Present Perfect → *already, yet, just, ever, never*
* Present Perfect Continuous → *for, since, all day, recently*

---

## 6. Past Simple vs. Past Perfect

### Past Simple

Describes a past event.

### Past Perfect

Shows that one past event happened **before another past event**.

> When I arrived, the class **had started**.

**Timeline:**

> Class started → I arrived

The earlier event uses **past perfect**.

Another example:

> She **had finished** her homework before she went to bed.

---

## 7. Talking About the Future

Different future forms express different meanings.

| Tense/Form             | Main Use                   | Example                           |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **will**               | Prediction/decision        | I will call you later.            |
| **be going to**        | Plan/intention or evidence | I am going to study tonight.      |
| **Present Continuous** | Fixed arrangement          | I am meeting my teacher tomorrow. |
| **Present Simple**     | Timetable/schedule         | The class starts at 8:00.         |

### Compare

> I **will** study tonight.
> → Decision/prediction

> I **am going to** study tonight.
> → Intention or plan

> I **am studying** with my friend tonight.
> → Arranged plan

---

## 8. Common Time Expressions

Time expressions often help you choose the correct tense.

| Expression    | Common Tense          |
| ------------- | --------------------- |
| every day     | Present Simple        |
| now           | Present Continuous    |
| yesterday     | Past Simple           |
| last week     | Past Simple           |
| while         | Often Past Continuous |
| already / yet | Present Perfect       |
| since / for   | Perfect tenses        |
| before        | Often Past Perfect    |
| tomorrow      | Future forms          |

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

When choosing between tenses, ask:

1. **When did the action happen?**
2. **Is it finished or still continuing?**
3. **Is the exact past time mentioned?**
4. **Did one past action happen before another?**
5. **Is the action a habit, happening now, or a future plan?**

### Quick Pattern

> **Habit/fact** → Present Simple
> **Happening now** → Present Continuous
> **Finished past time** → Past Simple
> **Past connected to present** → Present Perfect
> **Action in progress in the past** → Past Continuous
> **Earlier past action** → Past Perfect

---

## Quick Check

1. She usually ______ to class by bus.
   **Answer:** goes

2. Look! The students ______ football.
   **Answer:** are playing

3. I ______ my assignment yesterday.
   **Answer:** completed

4. I ______ my assignment already.
   **Answer:** have completed

5. When we arrived, the lecture ______.
   **Answer:** had started

---

> **💡 Remember:**
> Don't choose a tense only because of the verb. **Look at the time, meaning, and relationship between actions.**
> **Time + context = correct tense.**
