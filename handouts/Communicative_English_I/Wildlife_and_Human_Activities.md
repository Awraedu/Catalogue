# Wildlife and Human Activities

> **Core idea:** Human activities can either **protect wildlife or threaten it**. Understanding this relationship is important for conservation, sustainable development, and peaceful coexistence between people and wild animals.

## 1. What Is Wildlife?

**Wildlife** refers to animals, plants, and other living organisms that live naturally in the wild rather than being domesticated or controlled by humans.

Examples include:

* Elephants
* Lions
* Ethiopian wolves
* Gelada baboons
* Giraffes
* Wild birds
* Antelopes

Ethiopia has diverse wildlife and ecosystems, making wildlife an important natural resource and part of the country's tourism and cultural heritage.

---

# 2. Human Activities and Wildlife

Human activities affect wildlife in different ways.

Some activities are **harmful**, while others can support **conservation**.

### Harmful activities

* Deforestation
* Agricultural expansion
* Settlement expansion
* Overgrazing
* Poaching
* Illegal wildlife trade
* Pollution
* Mining and construction
* Unsustainable resource use
* Poorly managed tourism

### Helpful activities

* Wildlife conservation
* Habitat restoration
* Protected areas
* Reforestation
* Community conservation
* Environmental education
* Responsible tourism
* Wildlife monitoring

---

# 3. Habitat Destruction

A **habitat** is the natural environment where an organism lives.

When people clear forests, convert land to farms, construct buildings, or develop roads, wildlife may lose its habitat.

### Example

> **Forest clearing → habitat loss → fewer places for wildlife to live**

Habitat destruction is one of the major threats to wildlife in Ethiopia. Agricultural expansion, settlement, charcoal production, and other human activities can destroy or fragment wildlife habitats.

---

# 4. Agricultural Expansion

Agriculture is essential for human livelihoods, but expanding farms into natural habitats can reduce the space available to wildlife.

### Effects

* Loss of natural vegetation
* Destruction of animal habitats
* Reduced food sources
* Greater contact between people and wildlife
* Increased competition for resources

In Ethiopia, agricultural expansion is identified as one of the important drivers of human–wildlife conflict.

---

# 5. Deforestation

**Deforestation** is the removal of forests, often to obtain farmland, fuelwood, charcoal, or construction materials.

### Effects on wildlife

> **Deforestation → habitat loss → reduced resources → wildlife displacement**

Animals may be forced to move into new areas when their natural habitats disappear.

Deforestation is among the human activities associated with wildlife habitat destruction in Ethiopia.

---

# 6. Settlement and Urban Expansion

As human populations grow, settlements may expand into areas previously occupied by wildlife.

This can:

* Reduce wildlife habitat
* Fragment ecosystems
* Increase human–wildlife encounters
* Increase competition for resources

Research on human–wildlife conflict in Ethiopia identifies the proximity of settlements to protected areas and forests as an important driver of conflict.

---

# 7. Overgrazing

**Overgrazing** occurs when livestock graze an area so heavily that vegetation cannot recover properly.

### It can cause:

* Loss of vegetation
* Soil degradation
* Reduced food for wildlife
* Competition between livestock and wild animals
* Habitat degradation

Around Bale Mountains National Park, overgrazing has been identified among the causes associated with human–wildlife conflict.

---

# 8. Poaching and Illegal Wildlife Trade

**Poaching** is the illegal hunting or capturing of wild animals.

Animals may be killed for:

* Meat
* Skins
* Horns
* Ivory
* Traditional uses
* Illegal trade

Poaching reduces wildlife populations and can threaten endangered species.

> **Illegal hunting → fewer animals → population decline**

Illegal resource exploitation, including poaching, is identified as a driver of human–wildlife conflict in Ethiopia.

---

# 9. Pollution

Human pollution can affect wildlife and their habitats.

Sources include:

* Plastic waste
* Chemicals
* Oil
* Agricultural pesticides
* Industrial waste
* Sewage
* Air pollution

Pollution can contaminate:

> **Water → soil → food → wildlife**

Animals may become sick, lose food sources, or leave polluted habitats.

---

# 10. Roads and Development

Roads, buildings, dams, and other development projects can change wildlife habitats.

### Possible effects

* Habitat fragmentation
* Loss of vegetation
* Disturbance from noise
* Barriers to animal movement
* Vehicle–wildlife collisions

Development activities such as road construction and hydropower projects have been reported as contributors to wildlife habitat destruction in parts of Ethiopia.

---

# 11. Human–Wildlife Conflict

**Human–wildlife conflict** occurs when interactions between people and wild animals cause harm to people, wildlife, property, or livelihoods.

### Examples

Wild animals may:

* Destroy crops
* Kill livestock
* Damage property
* Threaten people

People may:

* Destroy animal habitats
* Kill problem animals
* Hunt wildlife
* Take natural resources from wildlife habitats

Research around Bale Mountains National Park documented crop damage, livestock predation, habitat destruction, and killing of wildlife and people as forms of human–wildlife conflict.

---

# 12. Why Does Human–Wildlife Conflict Increase?

A simple chain is:

> **Population growth → increased resource demand → habitat pressure → greater human–wildlife contact → conflict**

Important causes include:

* Agricultural expansion
* Settlement expansion
* Habitat loss
* Resource competition
* Overgrazing
* Deforestation
* Climate-related pressures
* Illegal resource use

These drivers have been identified in systematic research on human–wildlife conflict in Ethiopia.

---

# 13. Effects on People

Human–wildlife conflict can cause:

* Crop losses
* Livestock losses
* Property damage
* Economic losses
* Injuries
* Loss of human life
* Fear and insecurity

For rural communities that depend heavily on agriculture and livestock, these losses can have a particularly serious impact on livelihoods.

---

# 14. Effects on Wildlife

Wildlife can also suffer from human activities.

### Major effects

* Habitat loss
* Habitat fragmentation
* Reduced food and water
* Displacement
* Injury
* Population decline
* Reduced reproduction
* Increased mortality
* Risk of extinction

Human disturbance can also change the distribution, abundance, movement, and behavior of wild animals.

---

# 15. Positive Human Activities

Not every human activity is harmful.

People can actively protect wildlife through:

### Protected areas

National parks and other protected areas provide important habitats for wildlife.

### Reforestation

Planting and restoring forests can improve wildlife habitats.

### Community conservation

Local communities can participate in protecting wildlife and managing natural resources.

### Environmental education

Education can increase awareness and encourage responsible behavior.

### Wildlife monitoring

Monitoring populations helps conservationists understand changes and threats.

---

# 16. Tourism and Wildlife

Wildlife tourism can provide economic benefits and encourage conservation.

### Positive tourism

Responsible tourism can:

* Create employment
* Generate income
* Support local communities
* Increase conservation funding
* Raise awareness about wildlife

However, poorly managed tourism can disturb animals through excessive noise, vehicles, or close human contact. Research in Ethiopia has found that tourism disturbance can affect wildlife movement, foraging, and behavior.

> **Tourism can help wildlife when it is responsible and properly managed.**

---

# 17. Sustainable Use of Natural Resources

**Sustainable use** means using natural resources in ways that meet present needs without destroying their ability to support future generations.

### Example

Instead of:

> Cutting forests without limits

Use:

> **Controlled and sustainable forest management**

Instead of:

> Hunting endangered animals

Use:

> **Wildlife protection and regulated resource use**

---

# 18. How Can We Reduce Conflict?

Effective solutions can include:

* Protecting wildlife habitats
* Restoring degraded land
* Creating wildlife corridors
* Improving land-use planning
* Protecting crops and livestock
* Educating local communities
* Strengthening wildlife laws
* Preventing illegal hunting
* Supporting responsible tourism
* Involving local communities in conservation
* Sharing conservation benefits fairly

Research from Bale Mountains emphasizes community awareness, clear boundaries, appropriate rules, benefit sharing, and reducing settlement encroachment as possible approaches to coexistence.

---

# 19. Human–Wildlife Coexistence

**Coexistence** means people and wildlife living in the same broader landscape while reducing harmful conflict.

The goal is not simply:

> **“Keep humans away from wildlife.”**

A better goal is:

> **Protect wildlife + support communities + reduce conflict**

Successful conservation often requires understanding both **ecological needs** and **human livelihoods**.

# Quick Check

### 1. What is wildlife?

**Answer:** Wild animals, plants, and other organisms that live naturally rather than being domesticated.

### 2. What is habitat?

**Answer:** The natural environment in which an organism lives.

### 3. How does deforestation affect wildlife?

**Answer:** It destroys and fragments habitats and reduces available food and shelter.

### 4. What is poaching?

**Answer:** The illegal hunting or capturing of wild animals.

### 5. Give two examples of human–wildlife conflict.

**Answer:** Crop destruction and livestock predation.

### 6. How can agricultural expansion affect wildlife?

**Answer:** It can convert natural habitats into farmland and increase contact between humans and wildlife.

### 7. Is tourism always harmful to wildlife?

**Answer:** No. Responsible tourism can support conservation, although poorly managed tourism can disturb wildlife.

### 8. What is coexistence?

**Answer:** People and wildlife living in the same broader environment while reducing harmful conflict.

### 9. Give three ways to protect wildlife.

**Answer:** Protect habitats, prevent poaching, and involve local communities in conservation.

### 10. Complete:

> **Human activity → habitat change → ______ → human–wildlife conflict**

**Answer:** **increased human–wildlife contact**
