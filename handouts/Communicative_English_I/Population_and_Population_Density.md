# Population and Population Density

> **Core idea:** **Population** refers to the number of people living in a particular place, while **population density** describes how closely those people are distributed across a given area of land.

## 1. Population

**Population** is the total number of people or inhabitants living in a particular **country, region, city, or area**.

### Example

If 2 million people live in a region, the population of that region is:

> **2,000,000 people**

Population information can include:

* Population size
* Population distribution
* Population density
* Birth and death rates
* Migration
* Age and sex structure
* Population growth

---

## 2. Population Density

**Population density** is the number of people living in a particular unit of land area.

### Formula

$$
\text{Population Density} =
\frac{\text{Population}}{\text{Land Area}}
$$

Usually, population density is expressed as:

> **people per square kilometre (people/km²)**

### Example

A region has **500,000 people** and an area of **2,500 km²**.

$$
\frac{500,000}{2,500}=200
$$

Therefore:

> **Population density = 200 people/km²**

---

## 3. Dense and Sparsely Populated

### Densely populated

An area is **densely populated** when a relatively large number of people live in a small area.

> **High population density → many people per unit of land**

Examples may include large cities and urban centres.

### Sparsely populated

An area is **sparsely populated** when relatively few people live across a large area.

> **Low population density → few people per unit of land**

Examples may include deserts, remote areas, and some mountainous regions.

---

## 4. Population Distribution

**Population distribution** refers to **how people are spread across an area**.

Population is rarely distributed equally.

Some areas may have:

> **High concentration → cities and developed areas**

while others may have:

> **Low concentration → remote or physically difficult areas**

### Important distinction

| Term                        | Meaning                              |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Population**              | Number of people                     |
| **Population density**      | Number of people per unit of land    |
| **Population distribution** | How people are spread across an area |

> **Exam trap:** A country can have a **large population** but a **low population density** if it also has a very large land area.

---

## 5. Factors Affecting Population Density

Population density differs from one place to another because of several factors.

### Physical factors

* Climate
* Relief/topography
* Availability of water
* Soil fertility
* Natural resources

### Human and economic factors

* Employment opportunities
* Industry
* Transportation
* Education
* Health services
* Urbanization
* Historical development

### Example

A city with many universities, hospitals, businesses, and jobs may attract more people and therefore develop a **higher population density**.

---

## 6. Effects of High Population Density

High population density can create both **opportunities and challenges**.

### Possible advantages

* Large labour force
* Larger markets
* Better access to services
* More economic activity
* Efficient provision of some infrastructure

### Possible challenges

* Housing shortages
* Traffic congestion
* Pressure on water and food
* Waste-management problems
* Overcrowding
* Pressure on schools and hospitals
* Environmental pollution

---

## 7. Effects of Low Population Density

Low population density can also have advantages and disadvantages.

### Possible advantages

* Less overcrowding
* More available land per person
* Less traffic
* Lower pressure on some resources

### Possible challenges

* Limited access to services
* Fewer employment opportunities
* Higher cost of providing infrastructure
* Isolation of communities
* Greater difficulty connecting remote areas

---

## 8. Population Density and Natural Disasters

The effects of events such as **floods, fires, and diseases** can differ depending on population density.

In densely populated areas, a large number of people may be affected in a relatively small area.

In sparsely populated areas, fewer people may be exposed, although access to emergency services may be more difficult.

> **Important:** Population density does not automatically determine the severity of a disaster. Infrastructure, preparedness, geography, and access to services also matter.

---

## 9. Population Density in Ethiopia

Population density varies considerably across Ethiopia.

More people tend to be concentrated in areas with:

* Favourable environmental conditions
* Agricultural opportunities
* Cities and towns
* Transportation networks
* Employment and services

Less densely populated areas may be associated with:

* Arid environments
* Difficult terrain
* Limited water availability
* Remote locations

> **Exam point:** Population distribution is influenced by both **physical** and **socio-economic factors**.

## 10. Population Density vs. Population Size

These two concepts are easily confused.

### Population size

> **How many people?**

### Population density

> **How many people per unit of land?**

### Example

**Country A**

* Population: 10 million
* Area: 100,000 km²
* Density: 100 people/km²

**Country B**

* Population: 5 million
* Area: 10,000 km²
* Density: 500 people/km²

Country A has the **larger population**, but Country B has the **higher population density**.

---

## ⭐ Exam Focus

Remember these three definitions:

> **Population = total number of people**

> **Population density = population ÷ land area**

> **Population distribution = how people are spread across an area**

### Key formula

$$
D=\frac{P}{A}
$$

Where:

* **D** = density
* **P** = population
* **A** = area

### Key vocabulary

| Term                   | Meaning                                            |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dense**              | Crowded or closely packed                          |
| **Density**            | Amount of something within a particular space      |
| **Densely populated**  | Many people per unit area                          |
| **Sparsely populated** | Few people per unit area                           |
| **Distribution**       | The way people are spread across an area           |
| **Demography**         | The study of populations and their characteristics |

## Quick Check

1. What is population?
   **Answer:** The total number of people living in a particular place.

2. What is population density?
   **Answer:** The number of people living per unit of land area.

3. What is the formula for population density?
   **Answer:** **Population ÷ Land Area**

4. What is the difference between population and population density?
   **Answer:** Population is the total number of people, while population density measures people in relation to the size of the land area.

5. What does “sparsely populated” mean?
   **Answer:** An area contains relatively few people per unit of land.

6. Give two factors that affect population density.
   **Answer:** Climate and employment opportunities.

7. A town has 80,000 people and covers 400 km². What is its population density?

$$
\frac{80,000}{400}=\boxed{200\text{ people/km}^2}
$$

> **💡 Remember:**
> **Population tells you how many people there are. Population density tells you how crowded the land is. Population distribution tells you where those people are located.**
